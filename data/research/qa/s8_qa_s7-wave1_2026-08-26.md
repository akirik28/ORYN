## 2. REJECTED records — full detail

### S7-oryn4d-0014 — The Curieux Review — REJECTED (peer-review claim contradicted by its own cited source)

The record's `peer_review_explicit: false` and `peer_review_notes` state, with confidence:

> "Own site's own language does NOT use the term 'peer review' or 'peer-reviewed' anywhere in the submissions text retrieved... This describes in-house editorial vetting, not an external academic peer-review process."

I re-fetched the exact cited URL (`https://www.curieuxreview.com/submissions`) via WebFetch and independently confirmed via a direct browser render (`get_page_text`). The live page reads:

> "After submission, manuscripts enter a **peer review process** in which two of our editors separately review each article and decide whether it is suitable for publication."
> "Both pathways are **peer reviewed** by two separate editors and vetted for academic integrity..."

This is a direct, verifiable contradiction — the exact opposite of what the record asserts about its own cited source. This matters because the record's `notes_uncertainties` explicitly builds a caution ("recommend a second reviewer explicitly weigh the fee-for-publication model") on the false premise that the venue avoids peer-review language, when it in fact uses it twice on the same page it cites. Everything else on this record checked out accurately (age range MS/HS/UG, no country restriction, $200/$250 pay-on-acceptance fee, financial aid on request) — the fix is narrow (flip `peer_review_explicit` and rewrite `peer_review_notes`), not a full rewrite.

### S7-oryn4d-0042 — Türkiye Öğrenci Meclisi (Turkish Student Councils) — REJECTED (program appears defunct; regulatory basis repealed)

The record marks this `VERIFIED_ELIGIBLE`, deadline_status `annual_recurring_set_by_school_no_central_date`, citing as its sole official evidence a 2004 MEB directive PDF (`mevzuat.meb.gov.tr/dosyalar/286.pdf`) describing school→provincial→national student councils culminating in a session in the TBMM (Turkish Grand National Assembly) General Assembly hall.

Independent research found two separate, dated repeal events:

1. **2019 repeal of the founding directive.** Two independent Turkish news outlets (memurlar.net, kamudanhaber.net), both quoting what reads as the actual MEB cancellation notice, state:
   > "Milli Eğitim Bakanlığı Demokrasi Eğitimi ve Okul Meclisleri Yönergesi ilgi (a) Makam Oluru ile yürürlükten kaldırılmış ve bu yıl yapılması planlanan Türkiye Öğrenci Meclisi çalışmaları iptal edilmiştir." — "The Ministry of National Education's Democracy Education and School Councils Directive has been repealed by the referenced Ministerial Decision, and this year's planned Turkey Student Council activities have been cancelled."
   Dated 17 April 2019; the reported reason is that the TBMM Speaker's Office withdrew its partnership in 2013, and MEB continued the program alone until formally cancelling it in 2019. This is exactly the PDF/directive the record cites as its live evidentiary anchor.

2. **28 July 2026 repeal of the remaining regulatory hook** — one month before this research, and independently confirmed by me via a direct browser render of the full kamudanhaber.net article (not just a search snippet):
   > "28 Temmuz 2026 tarihli ve 33323 sayılı Resmî Gazete'de yayımlanan yönetmelik değişikliği kapsamında, yönetmeliğin 'Okul öğrenci meclisi' başlıklı 37. maddesi tamamen kaldırıldı." — "Under the regulation amendment published in the Official Gazette dated 28 July 2026, No. 33323, Article 37 ('School student council') of the [Pre-School Education and Primary Education Institutions] Regulation was entirely repealed."

**Honest caveat:** the July 2026 repeal specifically targets the *ilkokul/ortaokul* (primary/middle-school) regulation; I could not confirm or rule out a parallel provision for *ortaöğretim* (high school) or a province-run substitute (I found evidence of a distinct, still-active "İstanbul Öğrenci Meclisleri Projesi" run by the İstanbul Valiliği as recently as September 2025 school elections — a different, local, non-TBMM-linked initiative). So it's possible some form of in-school "öğrenci meclisi" title still exists locally under different authority. What appears clearly defunct is the specific *national* mechanism the record describes — provincial presidents convening in the TBMM General Assembly hall — which multiple sources say ended in 2019 and has no evidence of revival since. Recommend the coordinating session either drop this record or rewrite it around whatever provincial-level successor (if any) can be confirmed, rather than presenting the 2004 national directive as live.

## 3. Discrepancies flagged (kept at VERIFIED — core facts hold, but a specific claim is wrong)

### S7-oryn4d-0021 — The Concord Review — print-price sourcing reversed
`cost_notes` says the print edition is "$100 USD plus shipping... from the direct official-site fetch," calling this more authoritative than a "secondary source" figure of "$110 US / $150 international." My live re-fetch of the same URL (`tcr.org/submit`) shows the actual current official text is:
> "Author – Print US: $110.00 USD / Author – Print International: $150.00 USD"
The figure S7 downgraded as a mere secondary source is what the official page actually says. The primary `cost` field (70, eBook) is correct and unaffected. Deadlines (Aug 1/Nov 1/Feb 1/May 1), 5% acceptance rate, and "essays from 46 countries" all confirmed exactly.

### S7-oryn4d-0024 — John Locke Institute Essay Competition — late-fee figure wrong (two fees conflated)
`cost_notes` says the official page states "£10 for a 7-day extension." Direct browser render of the live FAQ:
> "there is a fee of 25.00 GBP (for the seven day extension) or 75.00 GBP (for the twenty-one day extension)."
There is a genuine "$10" on the page, but it's for a different thing — "Late registration deadline ($10): 30 April, 2026" — a late-*registration* fee in USD, weeks earlier than and distinct from the 7-day *submission* extension fee in GBP. S7 conflated two separate fees. Age limit (<19 as of 31 May 2026), "open to students from any country," free regular entry, and 2026 cycle closed — all confirmed exactly.

### S7-oryn4d-0028 — Polyphony Lit — "all submissions free" overclaimed
`cost_notes` states: "All submissions are free across every window and contest per the official guidelines page: 'all submissions free.'" Live fetch of `polyphonylit.org/submission-guidelines` directly contradicts the "no exceptions" framing:
> "For the Summer Contest, Fall Contest, and Winter Contest, the first 200 submissions will be free to submit... After we reach 200 submissions... there will be a $4 submission fee for additional submissions."
General Volume submissions and identity-focused contests (Black History, API Month, Pride, etc.) remain free; three seasonal contest windows do not, once they hit volume. Age range (14-18), worldwide eligibility, and editor-ineligibility all confirmed.

### S7-oryn4d-0048 — TKS (join.tks.world) — headline cost figure uncited/unconfirmed
`cost` is set to 7000 with a note that it's a "historic... per secondary source" figure and that the official page "did not surface a current headline number." I independently re-fetched the same official page and confirm: no dollar figure appears anywhere on it. So a structured numeric field carries a specific number the record's own evidence doesn't support. Contrast with the sibling record **S7-oryn4d-0059 (TKS Virtual)**, whose $5,890 figure ($1,000 deposit + $489 × 10 months) IS precisely and directly confirmed on `tks.world/virtual`. Recommend either caveating/removing the 0048 figure or pointing users to 0059's better-evidenced number. (Also flagging: 0048 and 0059 are close-to-overlapping records for the same underlying provider — one covers the general/in-person+virtual landing page, the other specifically the virtual track. Not a strict duplicate since in-person vs. virtual are materially different offers, but worth the coordinating session's eyes.)

### S7-oryn4d-0007 — Taiwan Scholarship (MOE) — eligibility text understates a nationality restriction
`eligibility_verbatim` is drawn from MOE's generic global eligibility ("Foreign national with at least a secondary school graduation diploma..."). But the actual application channel for a Turkey-based student — via the Taipei Mission in Ankara, the only route the record's own evidence cites — carries its own, more specific eligibility clause I found on the live page:
> "Applicants must be **a Turkish national** with a secondary school graduation diploma..."
A non-Turkish-national student living in Türkiye (e.g., a non-citizen at an international school) would likely not qualify through this specific channel, even though the record's generic "foreign national" framing doesn't surface that. Dates (1 Feb–31 Mar), NTD 40,000/semester tuition support, and NTD 15,000/month stipend all confirmed exactly.

### S7-oryn4d-0053 — Genç Kızılay — youth-branch age gate needs a second look

My independent fetch of the same official page found "Gençlik Kolları" membership described elsewhere with "18-35 yaşları arasında gençleri" (youth aged 18-35), which — if it's the same structure — would undercut the under-18 access route the record describes. I could not fully disambiguate whether school-based youth outreach clubs and the formal 18-35 Gençlik Kolları are the same or different structures. This reinforces, rather than resolves, S7's own pre-existing caution flag; treat as still needing the follow-up S7 already recommended.

## 4. Structural / recurring patterns (for the coordinating session)

1. **Cost/fee fields are the single most error-prone field type.** 4 of 41 VERIFIED records (0021, 0024, 0028, 0048 — ~10%) had a materially wrong or uncited number specifically in `cost`/`cost_notes`, while every other field on those same 4 records checked out accurately. Two of the four (0021, 0024) show the identical failure shape: S7 attributes a figure to "the direct official-site fetch" as more authoritative than a "secondary source," and live re-verification shows the figure S7 *downgraded* is actually what the official page says. Worth a dedicated re-fetch-and-requote pass on `cost_notes` fields specifically before production, rather than treating this as record-by-record noise.

2. **Turkey-access reasoning sometimes cites the generic/global eligibility text rather than the specific channel a Turkey-based student would actually use**, and the specific channel can be more restrictive (0007 Taiwan nationality requirement; 0053 Genç Kızılay age-gated Youth Branch). Worth a targeted second pass on any record where `evidence` cites a country-specific sub-page (an embassy, a mission, a national branch) rather than the organization's global page.

3. **S7's own self-flagged blocked sources were genuine, not a shortcut.** Every 403/cert-error/socket-hang-up I attempted to independently reproduce, reproduced (zonta.org, cogitojournal.org, nytimes.com — the last is blocked at the tool/environment level, not just a site quirk). Where a different tool (a real browser vs. WebFetch) got through on the same URL, content matched what S7 had synthesized from secondary sources closely — see §5. This suggests S7's honesty flags are well-calibrated and the coordinating session shouldn't discount CANDIDATE-tier records just because S7 couldn't personally load the page.
