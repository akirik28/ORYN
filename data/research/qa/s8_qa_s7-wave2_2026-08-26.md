Conflating the two materially misrepresents a hard geographic/institutional gate as optional — a Turkey-based applicant planning to enroll outside California would read this record as eligible when she is not. Everything else about the record is accurate; this one eligibility-framing issue is the sole but sufficient reason for REJECTED.

## S7-oryn4d-0010 — Global Korea Scholarship — Undergraduate Program — VERIFIED (upgrade substantiated, with a sourcing caveat)

**Specific question asked:** does the new evidence actually resolve the previously-flagged gap ("Turkey's per-country quota for the current cycle not confirmed on an official source")?

I pulled the cited PDF (`gksscholarship.com/.../Global-Korea-Scholarship-2026-Application-Guidelines.pdf`) and read it directly page-by-page (not just text-extracted). Findings:
- The document carries an authentic-looking cover: "2026 Global Korea Scholarship / Application Guidelines for Undergraduate Degrees," dated 2025.9, bearing the Republic of Korea Ministry of Education and National Institute for International Education (NIIED) logos.
- The Embassy Track country-quota table (page 4 of the PDF) lists, at row 62: **Turkey | General: 1 | R-GKS: 1 | Total: 2** — exactly matching the record's claim.
- The table is internally consistent: the General/Overseas-Korean/R-GKS columns sum correctly to the stated totals (82 + 7 + 61 = 150), matching the "150 scholars from 71 countries" figure stated in the surrounding text and elsewhere on the official `studyinkorea.go.kr` page. This internal consistency is a meaningful authenticity signal.
- **Gap:** `gksscholarship.com` is not an official government domain — its own homepage explicitly self-describes as an "**Unofficial guide for GKS Scholarship**" (a private aggregator/blog that links out to the real `studyinkorea.go.kr` for applications). The record's evidence array does label this correctly as `official_primary_mirror` (acknowledging it's a mirror), but the notes text ("pulled and read **the official** 2026 GKS-U Application Guidelines PDF directly") slightly overstates this — it was pulled from an unofficial mirror, not from a government-hosted copy, even though the document's content itself displays genuine government branding and passes an internal-consistency check.
- I could not locate or verify the notes' second claimed corroboration — "*a live studyinkorea.go.kr notice board post for the same programme/track*" — this source has no URL anywhere in the record, and I could not independently find it (attempted via WebFetch and Browser; the official site's live GKS overview page I did reach shows different, larger figures — 2,000 total scholars — that appear to combine graduate and undergraduate tracks with no per-country breakdown visible, so it neither confirms nor contradicts the Turkey-specific number; it's simply a different page/scope).

**Assessment:** the specific factual gap that justified the upgrade — whether Turkey has a real, non-zero quota — is substantively resolved by content I independently read and cross-checked for internal consistency, and nothing I found contradicts it. This is not a REJECTED-level defect. But the sourcing falls short of the rigor implied by a "VERIFIED_ELIGIBLE" label carrying a specific cited number: the primary new evidence rests on a self-described unofficial mirror rather than a `.go.kr` original, and a claimed second corroborating source is uncited and unlocatable. Recommend downgrading the confidence framing in the record's notes (from "the official... PDF" to "an unofficial mirror of the official PDF, content verified for internal consistency and official branding") and either sourcing the actual notice-board post URL or removing that specific corroboration claim if it can't be re-located.
