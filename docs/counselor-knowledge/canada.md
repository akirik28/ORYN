# Canada — counselor knowledge

Evidence base: 61 requirements across provincial-application-system records
(`data/research/university-requirements/ca_requirements_provincial-systems_2026-08-21.jsonl`) and
University of Waterloo (`ca_requirements_waterloo_2026-08-21.jsonl`) — no dedicated summary doc
exists for this country yet, so all VERIFIED claims below cite corpus record IDs directly — and
`docs/research/admissions-systems/canada.md` (SYSTEM-LEVEL BACKGROUND tier).

## Education is constitutionally provincial — there is no national admissions system, and centralization exists in only three provinces

**No pan-Canadian application platform exists.** Centralization exists only within three specific
provinces: **Ontario** (OUAC, near-universal — all Ontario universities except Royal Military
College process through it), **British Columbia** (EducationPlannerBC — UBC, SFU, UVic, UNBC and
others; "Sign up for a free EducationPlannerBC account and apply to any of B.C.'s 25 public
post-secondary institutions," REQ-2026-08-21-CA-0104, `VERIFIED_UNDATED`), and **Alberta**
(ApplyAlberta, linking 26 publicly funded institutions). **Quebec is structurally distinct from all
three**: CEGEP admission (the mandatory pre-university stage for Quebec residents) is centralized
regionally via SRAM/SRACQ/SRASL, but admission to the actual bachelor's degree is a **separate**,
university-specific application evaluated for CEGEP-origin applicants via the province-wide
**R-Score** (cote de rendement au collégial) — McGill's own page confirms: "Your application is
reviewed on your overall R-Score" (REQ-2026-08-21-CA-0114, `VERIFIED_UNDATED`). Manitoba,
Saskatchewan, Nova Scotia, New Brunswick, PEI, and Newfoundland & Labrador have **no centralized
platform found** — direct application is the norm there. **Never assume a Canada-wide application
system exists** — determine the target province first, since three genuinely different
centralization models (or none) apply depending on where the student is applying.

**No parallel-application restriction anywhere in Canada** — unlike UCAS's 5-choice cap, a student
may apply to as many universities across as many provinces as they can pay separate fees for, with
no exclusivity or single-choice mechanism.

## The same student's transcript is read three structurally different ways depending on where they apply — even within one university

McGill's own admission pages show this directly: an **Ontario** applicant is evaluated via an
Ontario Secondary School Diploma and McGill's own OSSD-specific standard
(REQ-2026-08-21-CA-0119, `VERIFIED_UNDATED`); a **Quebec** applicant with a Diplôme d'études
collégiales (DEC) is evaluated via the province-wide **R-Score** (REQ-2026-08-21-CA-0114); an
applicant from **any other Canadian province** is evaluated via that province's own diploma
standard, university-specific (REQ-2026-08-21-CA-0120, `VERIFIED_UNDATED`); an **international**
applicant is evaluated via "completion of the credentials that lead to university admission in
your country of study" (REQ-2026-08-21-CA-0121, `NEEDS_REVIEW`) — four genuinely different
evaluation mechanisms at one university, not a single Canadian standard applied everywhere.
**Admission averages/percentages, not a converted 4.0 GPA, are the dominant reporting convention**
at most Canadian universities — never force a Canadian percentage average into a US GPA scale.

## Quebec's English-proficiency exemption is grounded in schooling history, not nationality

McGill explicitly exempts Quebec applicants from proof of English proficiency if they "completed,
or are completing, a DEC at an English CEGEP" or an equivalent English-medium schooling history
(REQ-2026-08-21-CA-0116, `VERIFIED_HISTORICAL`) — the exemption tracks the language of instruction
actually completed, not citizenship or province of residence alone. A Quebec resident who completed
a French-medium CEGEP does not get this exemption automatically.

## Waterloo: AI-assisted supplementary-form review, and an essay-free admissions model by design

The **Admission Information Form (AIF)** is explicitly used **in place of** personal statements or
essays for Computer Science admission at Waterloo (REQ-2026-08-21-CA-0019, `VERIFIED_UNDATED`) — a
structurally different evidence model from the US's essay-centric approach, worth flagging clearly
to a student used to US application conventions who might over-invest in essay-style writing rather
than the AIF's own format. A genuinely notable operational fact: "The Admission Information Form
(AIF) is reviewed both by people who have been trained on reviewing supplementary information forms
and by artificial intelligence" (REQ-2026-08-21-CA-0023, `VERIFIED_UNDATED`) — worth knowing when
advising a student on how to approach the AIF, since it is read by more than one kind of reviewer.
The AIF is mandatory for all Engineering programmes (excluding Architecture), all Faculty of
Mathematics programmes, and Computer Science (REQ-2026-08-21-CA-0022) — not a universal Waterloo
requirement across every faculty.

## Waterloo Engineering is a separate-faculty admission model, not a university-wide one

Consistent with the cross-country matrix's RULE-ADMISSIONS-018 ("school-within-university"
admissions layers recur independently of a country's general admissions philosophy): Waterloo's
Engineering programmes each publish their own competitive admission-average range and approximate
intake separately (e.g. REQ-2026-08-21-CA-0007 through 0011 give per-programme ranges from "mid- to
high 80s" to "low to mid-90s," each with its own intake size, each explicitly noting "entrance
averages change slightly every year based on the competitiveness of the applicant pool that year").
**Never quote one Waterloo Engineering admission average as if it applies university-wide** — each
programme is a separate competitive pool with its own historical range, not a fixed cutoff.

Waterloo also accepts Grade 11 marks and mid-term/predicted Grade 12 grades to make a **conditional
offer of admission** (REQ-2026-08-21-CA-0003, `VERIFIED_UNDATED`) — a real predicted-grade
mechanism, contrasting with McGill's final-transcript-only approach and confirming
RULE-ADMISSIONS-006: whether predicted grades are used at all is genuinely institution-specific in
Canada, not a national policy either way.

## The TOEFL rescale trap recurs here too — Waterloo shows correct dated handling

Waterloo's own English-proficiency page states both the pre- and post-21-January-2026 TOEFL
thresholds explicitly, with the cutover date printed: "TOEFL iBT, for tests taken before January
21, 2026: 90 overall, with 25 in writing and 25 in speaking. TOEFL iBT, for tests taken from January
21, 2026..." (REQ-2026-08-21-CA-0027, `VERIFIED_UNDATED`) — a clean, correctly-dated handling of
the same rescale that trips up requirements elsewhere in this corpus (see the UK, Germany, and
Turkey docs for the failure mode). Worth citing as a positive example when explaining the rescale
concept to a student, alongside the negative examples found elsewhere.

## Applicant educated in Türkiye: a genuinely open research gap, not a confirmed simple or complex finding

University of Toronto's own country-requirement page structure confirms Turkey is a listed,
individually evaluated country, but the Turkey-specific row could not be retrieved this pass —
**flagged as an open item requiring direct manual verification per target university**, not filled
with confident inference. What can be said with more confidence, by pattern rather than direct
Turkey-specific sourcing: a strong MEB average alone is unlikely to be competitive for high-demand
programmes (Engineering, CS, Business) at top-ranked Canadian universities without strong subject
grades — the same subject-prerequisite pressure any international applicant faces; AP/IB/A-Level
materially strengthens both eligibility clarity and competitiveness, consistent with the pattern
already found for the US and UK; LGS/YKS play no found role in Canadian decisions (consistent with
the US/UK pattern). A UK-style named foundation-year pathway for MEB-only applicants was **not
confirmed as common** — Canada appears closer to the US pattern here, but this is inference, not a
directly sourced finding. **Do not present any Turkey-specific numeric threshold for a Canadian
university as confirmed** — none was found this pass.

## SYSTEM-LEVEL BACKGROUND: how the system works generally

- **No Canada-wide credential-recognition body.** Each university independently evaluates foreign
  secondary credentials against its own published country/qualification-specific minimums. WES
  (World Education Services) is used by some institutions and, more commonly, by
  immigration/professional-licensing bodies — it is **not** a universal or mandatory gateway for
  undergraduate admission; most large universities (U of T, McGill, UBC, Waterloo, Alberta)
  evaluate transcripts in-house against their own country-specific tables.
- Extracurricular weight is genuinely institution-dependent, not a national Canadian norm — U of
  T's general Arts & Science admission does not consider extracurriculars at all, while UBC's
  mandatory Personal Profile weighs them heavily (RULE-ADMISSIONS-008's own sharpest within-country
  example). Never apply one "Canada cares about X" assumption across universities.
- **Dominant counselor risk (per the cross-country matrix)**: transferring a UK (OUAC=UCAS) or US
  (essay-centric) mental model wholesale onto a system where provincial/university variation is
  the operative reality. OUAC is not a national UCAS-equivalent — it covers Ontario only, and nine
  other provinces/territories each run something structurally different or nothing centralized at
  all.
