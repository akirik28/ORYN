# Switzerland — counselor knowledge

Evidence base: national-framework requirements
(`data/research/university-requirements/es_ch_requirements_switzerland-system_2026-08-21.jsonl`)
plus ETH Zurich, EPFL, University of Geneva (UNIGE), University of Lausanne (UNIL), University of
Zurich (UZH) — no dedicated summary doc exists for this country yet, so all VERIFIED claims below
cite corpus record IDs directly — and `docs/research/admissions-systems/switzerland.md`
(SYSTEM-LEVEL BACKGROUND tier).

## There is no single Swiss admissions mechanism — at least four genuinely different ones coexist

Switzerland has no shared application platform of any kind, not even a Studielink-style
registration layer — each of roughly 12 universities/ETHs runs its own fully independent
application process. Recognition of a foreign school-leaving certificate is **not** a single
centralized decision either — swissuniversities publishes a shared reference list that member
institutions draw on, but "the relevant legal provisions of each university are decisive"
(REQ-2026-08-21-CH-0002, `VERIFIED_UNDATED`). On top of that baseline, **at least four structurally
distinct selection mechanisms operate side by side**, and a counselor must identify which one
applies before advising a student, not assume Switzerland has "a" Medicine gate:

1. **EMS (Eignungstest für das Medizinstudium)**, a centrally-administered aptitude test, required
   for Human Medicine/Dentistry/Veterinary Medicine/Chiropractic at Basel, Bern, Fribourg
   (German-language track), Zurich, USI, and ETH Zurich (REQ-2026-08-21-CH-0009,
   `VERIFIED_CURRENT`).
2. **No EMS at all**: Geneva (UNIGE), Lausanne (UNIL), and Neuchâtel explicitly do **not** apply a
   numerus clausus or the EMS at first-year Medicine entry (REQ-2026-08-21-CH-0010/CH-0411/CH-0510,
   all `VERIFIED_CURRENT`) — instead using a post-entry first-year competitive exam (the
   "concours") to gate progression into year 2, not entry into year 1.
3. **EPFL's new blanket capacity cap**: from Fall 2025, EPFL limits total first-year Bachelor
   intake to 3,000 students for at least four years (REQ-2026-08-21-CH-0108, `VERIFIED_UNDATED`) —
   a university-wide seat ceiling with no subject-specific EMS-style test, structurally different
   from the Medicine-only gates elsewhere.
4. **ETH Zurich's and EPFL's own bespoke entrance exams** for applicants whose foreign diploma
   isn't automatically recognized — see below, and distinct again from both EMS and the capacity
   cap.

**Never describe Switzerland's "numerus clausus" as one mechanism** — a student asking about
Medicine at Geneva faces a completely different structure than the identical question asked about
Zurich.

## Turkish applicants: only two Lise sub-types are named, and ETHZ has no exam-free path at all

**Only Anadolu Lisesi Diplomasi and Fen Lisesi Diplomasi are named as recognized** — confirmed
independently on swissuniversities' own country list and verbatim in ETH Zurich's own official
Admission Requirements PDF: *"Turkey (L): Upper secondary school-leaving certificate + certificate
of university admission in the desired academic subject provided by a recognised Turkish
university + reduced Entrance Examination ETHZ. Otherwise: Comprehensive Entrance Examination
ETHZ."* Other MEB Lise sub-types (Genel Lise, Meslek Lisesi, İmam-Hatip Lisesi) are simply **not
named** on either list — a genuine gap, not a confirmed exclusion (contrast Tilburg in the
Netherlands, which explicitly excludes Meslek/İmam-Hatip by name).

**At 10 of the 12 institutions**, the Lise diploma plus a certificate of admission to a 4-year
programme at a recognized Turkish university is stated as sufficient on its own — no ECUS, no
further exam. St. Gallen adds its own selection procedure on top regardless (applies to all
foreign-certificate holders, not Turkey-specific); USI adds unspecified "special conditions."

**At ETH Zurich, there is no exam-free pathway for this qualification at all** — even holding both
the Lise diploma *and* the Turkish-university-admission certificate still requires the **Reduced
Entrance Examination** (2 subjects: mathematics + one further subject, REQ-2026-08-21-CH-0202,
`VERIFIED_UNDATED`, conducted in German); without that admission certificate, the **Comprehensive
Entrance Examination** (8 subjects, REQ-2026-08-21-CH-0203) applies instead. **At EPFL, Turkey
falls under "a country that is not a member of EU, EFTA, nor UK"** — requiring EPFL's own entrance
exam (in French; CHF 150 registration + CHF 550/800 exam fee), with the CMS preparatory mathematics
course accepted as an equivalent alternative (REQ-2026-08-21-CH-0106, `VERIFIED_UNDATED`). **Never
tell a Turkish applicant "your diploma plus Turkish university admission is enough" without first
checking whether the target institution is ETHZ or EPFL** — both are named, real exceptions to the
10-institution norm, and both require an entrance exam regardless of documentation.

YKS's role is structurally implied but never directly named in any Swiss source: since Turkish
Lisans admission is itself YKS-gated, the "certificate of university admission" these institutions
require is presumably downstream of YKS — but no Swiss source cites a YKS score directly, so state
this as inference, not confirmed fact.

## Language of instruction is genuinely institution-specific, not a Bachelor's-level English default

Unlike Bachelor's programmes in the Netherlands or Germany's growing English-taught sector,
Switzerland's undergraduate teaching is overwhelmingly in German, French, or Italian — the
instruction language, not English. ETH Zurich: "The main teaching language in all Bachelor's degree
programmes is German" (REQ-2026-08-21-CH-0205, `VERIFIED_UNDATED`) — the entrance examination
itself is conducted in German (REQ-2026-08-21-CH-0204). UZH requires non-Swiss applicants to
demonstrate German at C1 (REQ-2026-08-21-CH-0304). EPFL requires French B2, with C1 "highly
recommended" (REQ-2026-08-21-CH-0105). UNIL requires French B1 minimum for non-native speakers
without a Swiss maturité (REQ-2026-08-21-CH-0505). **A student assuming Swiss universities teach in
English at the Bachelor's level, by analogy to Dutch/German patterns, will be wrong at every
institution in this corpus** — verify the specific teaching language before recommending Switzerland
to a student without French/German at the required level.

## Certificate recognition is genuinely conditional and diploma-type-specific, not a flat pass/fail

UNIGE's admission rules record real conditional-recognition shapes rather than one blanket
standard, verified against the authoritative PDF: Germany's Abitur is recognized only under
specific stated conditions (REQ-2026-08-21-CH-0402); France's post-2021-reform Baccalauréat général
is recognized only with specific subject conditions (REQ-2026-08-21-CH-0403); Hong Kong's HKDSE
requires a compound condition including 6 subjects at a specified format (REQ-2026-08-21-CH-0404);
IB requires the diploma to have been "followed continuously," with certain subjects (including
"littérature") explicitly excluded from counting toward UNIGE's recognition criteria
(REQ-2026-08-21-CH-0405/CH-0406). **These are not scalar thresholds** — they are structured,
diploma-type-specific conditions, and treating any of them as "just check the overall score" would
miss the real gate. UZH shows the identical pattern independently: a certificate that only
"partially" matches Swiss standards may still permit admission under stated conditions
(REQ-2026-08-21-CH-0302, `VERIFIED_UNDATED`, explicitly flagged in the corpus as "not a threshold").

**ECUS (the Swiss University Complementary Exam) applies broadly to non-Lisbon-Convention
countries**, but its trigger list is genuinely per-university — UNIGE names Egypt, Ethiopia,
Algeria, Morocco, Tunisia, Lebanon among others (REQ-2026-08-21-CH-0407); Turkey, as a Lisbon
Recognition Convention signatory, is not on this list. UNIL separately requires ECUS for any
applicant with an "unequivalent" foreign diploma, with four mandatory subjects (French,
mathematics, English, history — REQ-2026-08-21-CH-0506).

## SYSTEM-LEVEL BACKGROUND: how the system works generally

- No SAT/ACT-equivalent national test — EMS is the dominant Swiss-specific instrument, but as shown
  above it applies to specific institutions and specific fields (Medicine/Dentistry/Vet
  Med/Chiropractic), never as a general admissions test.
- Foreign secondary certificates from Lisbon Recognition Convention signatory countries get a
  presumption of recognition; non-signatory countries generally must also pass the ECUS complementary
  exam (REQ-2026-08-21-CH-0012, `NEEDS_REVIEW` — verify current signatory list before stating a
  specific country's status as settled).
- No native Swiss-Matura predicted-grade artifact was confirmed — cantonal Matura finals commonly
  sit May-June, after the commonly-cited ~30 April application deadline, suggesting even
  Swiss-track applicants may apply pre-results (unconfirmed system-wide). Foreign IB/A-Level/AP
  applicants do submit predicted/interim grades operationally in some cases (e.g. St. Gallen
  accepts a "half-year report" pending the final certificate).
- No shared numeric application-choice cap exists (no UCAS-5, Netherlands-2, or Germany-12
  equivalent) — a student applies to as many of the ~12 institutions as practical/financial capacity
  allows, each a fully separate fee and process. One partial exception: a single EMS sitting is
  usable across the whole EMS-requiring consortium.
- **Dominant counselor risk (per the cross-country matrix)**: assuming a diploma's presence on
  swissuniversities' country list, or a Turkish applicant's plain MEB Lise diploma, establishes
  admission on its own — it establishes eligibility at most, is frequently insufficient without the
  Turkish-university-admission certificate, and at ETHZ/EPFL specifically an entrance exam still
  applies even when every documentary condition is met. A second, equally live risk: assuming
  "numerus clausus" means one mechanism nationally, when Switzerland runs at least four genuinely
  different ones side by side (see above).
