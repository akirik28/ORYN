# Activity Progression Pathways

Answers: *what does a realistic, well-evidenced multi-year progression in a given area actually
look like, concretely — and how should ORYN recognize both a student following one and a student
who needs to start one late?* Named explicitly in the mission's "AFTER CORE PACKAGE" continuation
list ("investigate activity progression pathways") and not covered by any of the preceding 11
documents directly, though several ( `02`'s evidence-state ladder, `05`'s tier-aware redundancy
model, peer's `03` timing phases) supply the pieces this document assembles into concrete,
year-by-year worked examples.

## The abstract pathway shape, before any specific example

Every pathway example below follows the same four-stage shape, which is not this document's
invention — it is what `02`'s achievement-tier ladder and peer's `03` two-phase timing model
already imply when read together as a *sequence* rather than as two independent frameworks:

**Explore** (Phase 1, `02` tiers `registered`/`participated`) → **Commit** (narrowing to fewer
areas, `02` tier `participated` sustained over time, feeding `05`'s depth-dimension scoring) →
**Escalate** (`02` tiers `advanced`/`completed_project`, the `05` §4 "escalation" pattern
specifically — later, more selective, or more responsible versions of the same underlying
interest) → **Produce/Lead** (`02` tiers `award`/`publication`/`leadership_role`/`founder_role`,
Phase 2 of `03`, the stage application-readiness evidence should exist by).

**This is a model, not a mandate.** Section 5 addresses what a counselor should do for the (very
common, not at all penalized) student who doesn't follow this shape cleanly.

## Pathway 1 — STEM/research interest

| Year | Stage | Concrete example | Evidence-tier (`02`) | Timing phase (`03`) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (grade 9) | Explore | Strong coursework, a general school science club, first exposure to a subject via a free online course | `participated` | Phase 1 |
| 2 (grade 10) | Explore→Commit | A local/regional science fair entry (no placement expected or required — per `06/10`'s medicine-family finding and `02`'s calibration, most entrants at this stage don't place, and that's fine); narrowing from "science broadly" to one sub-field | `participated`, maybe `completed_project` | Phase 1 |
| 3 (grade 11) | Escalate | Applying to and attending a structured, mentored summer research program | `advanced` (selective admission itself is evidence) → `participated`/`completed_project` | Phase 1→2 transition |
| 4 (grade 12) | Produce/Lead | Writing up the prior summer's work (poster/paper/presentation), possibly entering a competition with it, possibly mentoring a younger student in the same school club | `publication`/`presentation` tier, possibly `leadership_role` | Phase 2 |

**Why Year 3 lands specifically at grade 11, sourced, not assumed:** the **Simons Summer Research
Program** (Stony Brook University, official program) states plainly, with "no exceptions," that
applicants must be *current 11th-grade juniors* at the time of application. (Source:
`stonybrook.edu/commcms/simons`, official program site — high confidence.) [[RULE-COUNSEL-069]]
This is not an arbitrary example — peer's own `03` §3 independently found the same pattern across
multiple named programs (Harvard Pre-College, Stanford SIMR, Davidson Research Initiative, and
others) clustering around "rising junior/senior" eligibility, which this document's direct
verification of one specific program (Simons) confirms with primary-source precision rather than
only the aggregated-pattern confidence peer's own sourcing table assigned it. **Concrete product
implication**: a grade-9 or grade-10 student whose profile shows no structured summer-research
participation yet is not exhibiting a gap by this pathway's own logic — most of the flagship
programs literally cannot admit them yet. A gap-detection system unaware of this would misfire
specifically in a way this document exists to prevent.

## Pathway 2 — Entrepreneurship/business interest

| Year | Stage | Concrete example | Evidence-tier | Timing phase |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (grade 9) | Explore | Member of a business/DECA-style club, or informally helping with a family/friend's small venture | `participated` | Phase 1 |
| 2 (grade 10) | Commit | A small, real, self-directed side activity (freelance service, a small sale-based project) — real per `06/09-11`'s (business/entrepreneurship family, peer-owned) own likely emphasis on real vs. simulated evidence, cross-referenced from this document rather than restated | `participated`, `completed_project` | Phase 1 |
| 3 (grade 11) | Escalate | Either a genuine founder role (per `02`'s `founder_role` tier, evaluable via `entrepreneurship.ts`'s existing founder/revenue detection) or an officer/leadership role in the earlier club, whichever the student's actual interest points toward — **this is a real fork, not a single correct path**, and a counselor should not treat "must found something" as the only valid escalation | `founder_role` or `leadership_role` | Phase 2 |
| 4 (grade 12) | Produce/Lead | Demonstrated outcome (revenue, users, a measurable organizational result) or a business-plan competition entry using the venture as real material rather than a hypothetical pitch | `award`/measurable-outcome tier | Phase 2 |

**Redundancy-model worked example**: a student who was a DECA member in grade 9, a DECA officer in
grade 10, DECA chapter president in grade 11, and DECA president again in grade 12 (same
organization, same-or-similar role, no new scope) is the **lateral-repeat** pattern `05` §4 warns
about, even though every year shows a "leadership" activity — the redundancy is in the *lack of
escalating scope*, not in the mere repetition of the organization. Compare to a student who was a
DECA member in grade 9, chapter officer in grade 10, then **founded** an unrelated small venture in
grade 11 using skills DECA helped build — this is the `05` §4 **escalation** pattern (different
activity, but a genuine capability progression) and should not be redundancy-discounted the way the
first student's grade 12 repeat presidency should be. [[RULE-COUNSEL-070]]

**Added on a follow-up pass, giving this pathway the same named-program grounding Pathways 1 and 3
already had**: DECA's own official competitive-events page names specific entrepreneurship events
directly relevant to this pathway — **Start-Up Business Plan, Independent Business Plan, Franchise
Business Plan, International Business Plan, Innovation Plan**, and **Entrepreneurship Series** —
distinct competitive categories, not one generic "business competition." (Source:
`deca.org/compete`, official — high confidence, directly fetched and quoted for the specific event
names.) Competition runs through **chartered-association/district, state, and international
(ICDC — International Career Development Conference)** levels (Source: DECA-guidance sources
describing this tier structure — medium-high confidence, not independently re-confirmed by this
session's own direct fetch of a tier-specific page). This gives the redundancy worked-example above
a concrete evidence-tier hook: a grade-11 escalation into a *specific named event* (e.g.
progressing from chapter-level "Principles of Entrepreneurship" to district/state qualification in
"Start-Up Business Plan") is directly analogous to Pathway 1's finalist/semifinalist tier
distinctions, not merely "did more DECA." [[RULE-COUNSEL-123]]

## Pathway 3 — Creative/visual arts interest

| Year | Stage | Concrete example | Evidence-tier | Timing phase |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1-2 (grades 9-10) | Explore | Broad medium sampling (per `06/17`'s finding that medium breadth is part of this family's own evidence norm, not in tension with depth) — sketching, photography, digital art, whatever is accessible | `participated` | Phase 1 |
| 2-3 (grade 10-11) | Commit→Escalate | Submitting to Scholastic Art & Writing Awards regionally (per `06/15` §2 — most submissions don't medal, and that's calibrated-for, real evidence of sustained work regardless) | `participated`/`award` (if regional recognition occurs) | Phase 1→2 |
| 4 (grade 12) | Produce/Lead | A focused 10-12 piece portfolio (per `06/17`'s National Portfolio Day sourcing) narrowed to 1-2 media, process documentation included | `completed_project` at portfolio scale | Phase 2 |

**This pathway's escalation looks different from Pathways 1-2** — the "escalation" here is
*narrowing* (from many media to a focused portfolio) rather than *advancing through gated program
tiers*. Worth naming explicitly: **not every pathway's escalation step looks like "got into a more
selective program"** — for portfolio-based fields, escalation is often self-directed curation and
depth within work the student already controls, with no external gatekeeper required at all
(reinforcing `06/16`'s and `06/17`'s shared finding that this family has an unusually low access
barrier to its core evidence type). [[RULE-COUNSEL-071]]

## What happens to a student who starts a pathway late, or never follows one cleanly

**This is the more common real case, and the framework must handle it as well as it handles the
clean examples above.** Directly connects to peer's `03` §5 exceptions (a Phase-2 student who is
still broad because of a genuine, recent interest shift should have that context surfaced, not be
penalized) and this session's `04` §4 (access-aware gap checks). A grade-11 student newly
discovering an interest has, by pathway logic, "missed" Years 1-2 — the honest counselor response
is a **compressed pathway**, not a flagged deficiency: skip straight to Commit-stage activities
sized to the remaining runway, be explicit that depth now matters more than the breadth-first
sequence Pathway 1-3's early years modeled, and *do not* recommend the student try to retroactively
demonstrate Years 1-2 (that time has passed; recommending it wastes the student's actual remaining
runway chasing a sequencing ideal rather than building real evidence with the time that's left).
[[RULE-COUNSEL-072]] This is a direct, concrete specification of what
`docs/counselor-core-plan.md` §5's "with limited time before applications, prioritize breadth over
starting something new" principle should mean in practice for a late-discovered interest
specifically — that existing design principle already pointed the right direction; this document
supplies the worked mechanism.

## Rules established in this document

- `RULE-COUNSEL-069` — Treat "rising junior/senior" (roughly grade 11 application, grade 12
  program) as a well-evidenced, officially-confirmed eligibility pattern for flagship structured
  research programs; a younger student's absence of this specific evidence type is expected, not a
  gap. Confidence: high (direct official-source verification of one representative program, Simons
  Summer Research; consistent with peer's own broader-but-lower-confidence pattern finding across
  multiple programs).
- `RULE-COUNSEL-070` — Evaluate redundancy by whether scope/responsibility escalated across years
  within a pathway, not merely by whether the student stayed in the "same" organization or
  switched to a new one — same-organization escalation and cross-organization escalation are both
  legitimate; same-organization stagnation is the actual redundant pattern. Confidence: high
  (direct extension of `05`'s tier-aware model with a concrete worked case).
- `RULE-COUNSEL-071` — Not every pathway's "escalation" stage requires an external, gated,
  more-selective program — self-directed curation/narrowing is a legitimate escalation mechanism
  for portfolio-based creative fields specifically. Confidence: medium-high (reasoned from `06/16`
  and `06/17`'s independently-converging access-barrier findings).
- `RULE-COUNSEL-072` — For a student discovering an interest late relative to a pathway's typical
  timeline, recommend a compressed, depth-first pathway sized to actual remaining time — never
  recommend retroactively demonstrating skipped early-pathway stages. Confidence: high (direct,
  concrete specification of an existing architectural principle from
  `docs/counselor-core-plan.md` §5).
- `RULE-COUNSEL-123` — DECA's own official competitive-events page names specific, distinct
  entrepreneurship events (Start-Up Business Plan, Independent Business Plan, Franchise Business
  Plan, International Business Plan, Innovation Plan, Entrepreneurship Series) rather than one
  generic "business competition" — gives Pathway 2 the same named-program evidence-tier hook
  Pathways 1 and 3 already had. Confidence: high for the specific event names (official source,
  directly fetched); medium-high for the district/state/international tier structure (not
  independently re-confirmed by this session's own direct fetch of a tier-specific page).
