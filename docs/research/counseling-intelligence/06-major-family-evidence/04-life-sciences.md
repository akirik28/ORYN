# 06.04 — Life Sciences

Covers the mission's "biology" raw field plus the *research-science* reading of "neuroscience"
(molecular/cellular/systems neuroscience as a life-science research field) and biochemistry.
**Explicit boundary with the peer session's family 11** (Psychology & Behavioral Science): the
*behavioral/cognitive/clinical* reading of neuroscience belongs there; the *molecular/cellular/
systems biology* reading belongs here. A student interested in "how the brain works" may
legitimately fit either or both — this is a real interdisciplinary case (see §5), not a filing
error to resolve by picking one family. Onboarding anchor: **Biology**.

## 1. Field identity and subfields

Life sciences range from molecular/cellular biology (biochemistry, genetics, cell biology) to
organismal/ecological biology (physiology, ecology, evolutionary biology) to biomedical-adjacent
research (the non-clinical research side of what eventually supports medicine — distinct from
clinical practice itself, covered in the peer session's family 10). **RULE-COUNSEL-041:** "biology"
as a stated interest spans wet-lab bench work, field/ecological work (very different day-to-day
activity, often outdoors and observational), and computational biology (see §5) — probing which of
these a student actually means matters as much here as the analogous probes already established in
`01-computing-information-sciences.md` and `03-physical-sciences.md`.

## 2. Core skills

Per BLS's occupational profiles for biochemists/biophysicists and biological technicians: analytical
skills for conducting experiments and evaluating results, technical skill in operating laboratory
instrumentation, and — again, a recurring pattern across every science family checked in this
research — communication and teamwork, since BLS explicitly names the ability "to communicate and
work with a team" as essential [S-BLS-BIOCHEM]. BLS also notes that independent research roles in
biochemistry/biophysics typically require a Ph.D., while entry-level roles are reachable with a
bachelor's or master's [S-BLS-BIOCHEM] — a useful, concrete data point for a student weighing how
far they'd need to go in formal education to reach different levels of a life-sciences research
career, without this package overstating it into a universal claim about every life-science role
(e.g., many biology-degree holders go into non-research roles entirely, see §3).

## 3. Career families

Beyond academic/industry research (pharmaceutical and biotech R&D, agricultural science): biology
degrees are also a common route into science communication/education, environmental/conservation
work (see `09-environmental-science-sustainability.md`), regulatory/policy roles (e.g., health or
environmental regulatory agencies), and — the most numerically significant single destination in
many countries — as a pre-requisite pathway toward medicine or other clinical health professions
(see the peer session's `10-medicine-clinical-pathways.md`) without itself being a guarantee of
admission to those professional programs. **RULE-COUNSEL-042:** a biology major is necessary for
essentially no clinical health profession in most systems (pre-med "requirements" are typically a
set of specific courses, not a mandated major — many successful medical-school applicants in the
US, for instance, major in unrelated fields and complete pre-med coursework alongside) — this must
be stated explicitly to counteract a very common, real misconception this research repeatedly
encountered in secondary sources during this session's searches (not one this package cites as
evidentiary, per its own source-discipline, but a pattern real enough to flag and correct).

## 4. Exploration pathways by stage

**Phase 1:** an accessible hands-on project (a school-supervised experiment, a citizen-science data
contribution, a nature-observation/field project), general coursework engagement.

**Phase 2:** the **International Biology Olympiad (IBO)** — national-olympiad winners from 70+
countries compete in a multi-day exam combining theoretical assessment and hands-on practical
exams (animal anatomy, plant biology, bioinformatics) [S-IBO] — a strong, internationally
comparable evidence point structurally similar to the chemistry olympiad in
`03-physical-sciences.md` §4. Beyond competition: a sustained lab-based or field-based research
project (ideally with a named mentor and a described methodology, per
`01-development-taxonomy.md`'s `research` section and its sub-facet ladder), or a structured pre-college
research/lab-exposure program — genuinely valuable *exposure*-stage evidence even without a
publication, per that same section's caution against requiring publication for a strong signal.

## 5. Interdisciplinary connections

**Biology + CS** (computational biology/bioinformatics — explicitly named in the IBO's own practical
exam content [S-IBO]): see `01-computing-information-sciences.md` §5 for the reciprocal framing; a
project combining a real biological dataset with actual computational analysis is stronger combined
evidence than either a pure wet-lab or pure coding project alone for a student with this specific
combination of interests. **Biology + chemistry** (biochemistry): see `03-physical-sciences.md`.
**Biology + neuroscience-as-behavior** (cognitive science): see the peer session's
`11-psychology-behavioral-science.md` — flagged here explicitly so the connection isn't lost if that
document is drafted independently of this one. **Biology + environmental science** (ecology,
conservation biology): see `09-environmental-science-sustainability.md`.

## 6. Professional/licensure notes

**No general licensure requirement** for biological-science research roles themselves. The
licensure-heavy destination adjacent to this family is **clinical practice** (medicine, veterinary
medicine, etc.) — covered under the peer session's family 10, not this one; this family is
explicitly the non-clinical, research-science reading of the life sciences, per the scope note at
the top of this document.

## 7. Country notes (UK / EU / Turkey)

**United Kingdom:** National Careers Service's official **Biologist** profile describes tasks
spanning crop-production/medical-research trial work, endangered-species conservation, disease
research, and scientific publishing [S-NCS-BIO] — usefully broader than a single BLS occupation
code, since it reads across what this document's §1 already calls out as the family's real
internal split (molecular/cellular vs. organismal/ecological). No licensure gate applies.

**EU:** ESCO covers life-science occupations across EU languages (see
`01-computing-information-sciences.md` §7). No EU-wide licensure gate applies to non-clinical
life-science roles.

**Turkey:** no field-specific official source independently verified this pass. Flagged in
`10-open-questions.md`.
