# Major-Family Evidence: Political Science, International Relations & Public Policy

Scope: political science, international relations, public policy, government/civics-focused
interest.

## Model UN as the dominant, official-adjacent evidence channel — and its real limits

Model United Nations is the most common structured activity in this family, run under guidance
directly from the UN itself (the UN organized Global MUN conferences in 2009-2011 that produced
updated official Rules of Procedure, and publishes its own MUN resource guides). Official UN
guidance for delegates stresses that **arguments should be backed by evidence — data, historical
precedent, treaties, statistics — aligned with a country's real-world position**, not merely
performed debate skill. (Source: `un.org/en/model-united-nations`, official UN pages — high
confidence.) [[RULE-COUNSEL-038]]

**The limit worth naming explicitly**: MUN measures simulated argumentation and research under a
structured format — real, legitimate evidence of `intellectual_curiosity` and (for
officer/chair roles) `leadership`, but it is a simulation, not equivalent to engagement with
actual policy processes. A student's profile in this family is strongest when MUN-style evidence
is paired with *something real-world*: a campaign or local-government internship, testimony/
public-comment participation, policy-focused writing published anywhere (school paper, a
personal blog with real analytical content, a local newspaper op-ed), or civic-organization
volunteering. Treating a strong MUN record as sufficient on its own would overweight simulated
skill relative to the real-world engagement this field's actual work involves.
[[RULE-COUNSEL-039]]

## Mapping onto the shipped taxonomy

- `intellectual_curiosity` / `research` — MUN preparation, policy research/writing, debate.
- `leadership` — MUN committee chair/officer roles, founding or leading a school
  politics/civics club — subject to `01`'s existing "title alone is weak" caution just as much as
  any other leadership claim.
- `career_exploration` — internships with campaigns, government offices, NGOs, think tanks (the
  "real-world" evidence §1 argues matters most for differentiation).
- `community_impact` — civic-engagement volunteering (voter registration drives, local advocacy).

## Unsafe inferences specific to this family

- **Do not treat a MUN "Best Delegate" award as equivalent evidence to real policy engagement** —
  per §1, it demonstrates simulated argumentation skill, which is real but narrower than the field
  itself; an explanation citing this award should describe it accurately (strong
  research/argumentation under a competitive format) rather than implying broader policy expertise.
- **Do not assume access to campaign/government internships is universal** — these are frequently
  geography-gated (proximity to a capital, a campaign office, an NGO) and sometimes age-gated;
  absence of this specific evidence type should not be read as weak interest for a student without
  reasonable geographic access, consistent with `04-profile-gap-framework.md`'s access-aware gap
  model. [[RULE-COUNSEL-040]]

## Country notes (UK / EU / Turkey)

Career-outlook context throughout, not admissions-evidence guidance — see
`10-medicine-clinical-pathways.md` for the full method/bounding statement.

**United Kingdom:** no single NCS job profile cleanly represents this family (it maps onto many
possible roles — civil service policy roles, diplomacy, NGO/think-tank work, elected politics —
rather than one named occupation the way medicine or law does); NCS's broader "government and
legal" and "charity and volunteering" job-sector browse categories are the closer official
starting point, not independently reviewed page-by-page this pass.

**EU:** ESCO covers public-policy/international-relations-adjacent occupations across EU languages
(see `10`'s EU note for ESCO's full description) — again, more a browsable classification than a
single named profile for this diffuse family.

**Turkey:** checked against İŞKUR's official "Geleceğin Meslekleri" list (see
`10-medicine-clinical-pathways.md` for the full sourcing/bounding statement) — **no direct match
found**. The list is skewed toward technical/vocational/engineering roles rather than white-collar
social-science professions, so its absence here says nothing about this field's real relevance in
Turkey, only that this particular government list doesn't happen to name it. Written down honestly
as a gap rather than forced into a weak citation.

## Rules established in this document

- `RULE-COUNSEL-038` — Treat official UN MUN guidance (evidence-based argumentation, real-world
  policy grounding) as the authoritative description of what strong MUN participation actually
  requires, useful for distinguishing genuine engagement from surface participation. Confidence:
  high (official source).
- `RULE-COUNSEL-039` — Do not treat MUN achievement alone as sufficient evidence for this family;
  pair with real-world engagement evidence where recommending next steps. Confidence: medium-high
  (reasoned from the simulation-vs-reality distinction, not a specific cited study).
- `RULE-COUNSEL-040` — Do not read absence of campaign/government/NGO internship evidence as a
  motivation gap without considering geographic access. Confidence: high (direct application of
  the general access-aware gap principle from `04`).
