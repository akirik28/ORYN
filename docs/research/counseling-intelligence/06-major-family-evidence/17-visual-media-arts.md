# Major-Family Evidence: Visual & Media Arts

Scope: fine arts (painting, drawing, sculpture, photography), film/video production, digital
media arts. Distinct from `16-architecture-design.md` (spatial/functional design) though evidence
practices overlap substantially.

## Official portfolio-review infrastructure exists at national scale

**National Portfolio Day** is a long-running, official, nationwide series of free events where
students present work directly to admissions representatives from accredited art/design colleges
and receive real-time feedback before formal application. Standard visual-art portfolio guidance
from these events: **10-12 pieces of best, recent work**, physical or digital, and — again,
consistent with `16`'s finding — explicit openness to **sketchbooks and works-in-progress**, not
only polished final pieces; being able to discuss creative process is named as comparably important
to the work itself. (Source: National Portfolio Day host-institution pages — high confidence for
describing this specific, real, official event and its standard guidance.) [[RULE-COUNSEL-050]]

## Film specifically: a genuinely important equity/access finding

Guidance describing what strong undergraduate film-program portfolios actually look for is
unusually explicit on an access point directly relevant to this whole research package's
unsafe-inference concerns: reviewers focus on **storytelling clarity, visual composition, and
editing choices — not expensive equipment or professional-level production value**; **"simple
concepts executed well often outperform ambitious projects that lack focus,"** and shorter, focused
submissions are frequently more effective than longer, more resource-intensive ones. (Source:
film-program-admissions-guidance sources describing reviewer practice — medium-high confidence.)
[[RULE-COUNSEL-051]] **This directly refutes a specific, plausible unsafe inference this package
flags explicitly: that a student without access to professional film equipment has weaker
evidence potential in this family than a student who does.** A phone-shot, well-edited three-minute
short is legitimate, strong evidence; ORYN should never imply otherwise, and should never
recommend "get better equipment" as a priority fix ahead of "tighten the story/edit."

## Mapping onto the shipped taxonomy

- `execution_project_depth` — the primary dimension; a finished short film, a coherent body of
  visual work, a completed personal project all map directly.
- `intellectual_curiosity` — cross-medium experimentation, consistent with `16`'s finding that
  medium breadth is part of this family's own evidence norm, not in tension with depth.
  `awards_distinction` — National Portfolio Day itself is feedback-oriented, not competitive/
  tiered, so it should not be treated as an awards-type evidence source; Scholastic Art & Writing
  Awards (`15-literature-journalism-communication.md`) remains the tiered-competition channel that
  also accepts visual-art categories, worth cross-referencing rather than duplicating here.

## Unsafe inferences specific to this family

- **Do not imply better equipment or production value strengthens evidence more than storytelling/
  composition/editing craft** — the §2 finding is specific and should directly shape any
  explanation text touching film evidence. [[RULE-COUNSEL-051]] (restated for emphasis as the
  family's clearest, most actionable unsafe-inference guard)
- **Do not treat a sketchbook or in-progress work as lesser evidence than a "finished" piece** —
  per §1, explicitly welcomed by the official portfolio-review standard itself.

## Country notes (UK / EU / Turkey)

Career-outlook context throughout, not admissions-evidence guidance — see
`10-medicine-clinical-pathways.md` for the full method/bounding statement.

**United Kingdom:** no single NCS profile represents "fine artist" or "filmmaker" cleanly this
pass; NCS's "creative and media" job-sector category is the closer official starting point, not
individually reviewed page-by-page.

**EU:** ESCO covers creative-arts/media occupations across EU languages (see `10`'s EU note for
ESCO's full description).

**Turkey:** checked against İŞKUR's official "Geleceğin Meslekleri" list — the closest entries are
**Grafiker** (Graphic Designer) and **Teknik Ressam** (Technical Draftsman), a moderate but not
strong match: both are commercial/technical design roles rather than fine-art, film, or media-art
specifically as this document means them. Noted as a partial, honestly-bounded match rather than
either a full citation or a silent gap.

## Rules established in this document

- `RULE-COUNSEL-050` — Treat National Portfolio Day's standard guidance (10-12 best/recent pieces,
  process-inclusive, feedback-oriented rather than competitive) as the reference model for visual-
  art evidence expectations. Confidence: high (official, real event).
- `RULE-COUNSEL-051` — Never suggest production value/equipment quality is a priority evidence gap
  for film/media work; storytelling, composition, and editing are what reviewers name as
  differentiating. Confidence: medium-high (consistent guidance across sourced material, not a
  single primary-authority citation).
