# Major-Family Evidence: Architecture & Design

Scope: architecture, industrial/product design, interior/spatial design interest.

## Portfolio is the evidence standard, and it is explicitly process-inclusive

Architecture-program admissions guidance is portfolio-centered by design: representative examples
call for **10-15 pages of visual work** (or, for advanced-standing applicants, 15-25 discrete
projects) that may include "assignment-based projects, self-directed work... multimedia work,
photography, drawings, sketches, collages, models, paintings, sculpture" — i.e., a portfolio is
explicitly not limited to finished architectural drawings. Programs state directly that they want
to see **design thinking and process**, not only polished final output — being ready to explain
"how you develop ideas and where you want to take them" is named as comparably important to the
final piece itself. (Source: architecture-program admissions/portfolio-requirement pages — high
confidence for describing common program-level practice; specific page-count/project-count numbers
vary by institution, cited here as representative, not universal.) [[RULE-COUNSEL-048]] The
**National Architectural Accrediting Board (NAAB)** is the official accrediting body for
professional architecture degrees in the US, useful mainly as an authority marker for which
programs are professionally recognized, not as a source of high-school-stage evidence guidance
directly (NAAB accredits degree programs, not individual student portfolios).

## Mapping onto the shipped taxonomy

- `execution_project_depth` — the portfolio itself is, almost by definition, evidence for this
  dimension: process documentation, iteration, and a range of media all map directly onto what
  `execution.ts` already rewards (outcome description, shipped/tangible artifacts).
- `intellectual_curiosity` — cross-media exploration (a portfolio spanning photography, drawing,
  digital, and physical model work) is explicitly valued, not penalized as unfocused — an important
  exception to this package's general "depth over breadth" finding (`01` §2): for this specific
  family, breadth of *medium* within a design practice is itself part of the evidence standard, not
  in tension with it.
- `academics` — math/physics coursework (structural relevance) is named as relevant but secondary
  to portfolio strength.

## Unsafe inferences specific to this family

- **Do not assume architecture/design evidence requires access to specialized studio space,
  professional software, or expensive materials.** The sourced portfolio guidance explicitly
  welcomes sketches, collages, and low-cost media alongside digital/professional work — a
  access-aware finding worth stating plainly for `04-profile-gap-framework.md` purposes: a thin
  portfolio is not automatically an access problem the way, say, a paid summer architecture program
  might be, since the core evidence (sketchbooks, drawings, self-directed design projects) has a
  comparatively low material cost barrier. [[RULE-COUNSEL-049]]
- **Do not treat unfinished/in-progress work as invalid evidence** — explicitly named as acceptable
  portfolio content by the sourced guidance ("works in progress" alongside finished pieces).

## Rules established in this document

- `RULE-COUNSEL-048` — Treat architecture/design evidence as portfolio-and-process-centered;
  process documentation (sketches, iterations, works-in-progress) is legitimate evidence on par
  with finished pieces. Confidence: high (consistent program-level guidance).
- `RULE-COUNSEL-049` — Do not assume a thin design portfolio reflects an access barrier the way a
  paid program would — the core low-cost evidence channel (sketching, self-directed design work)
  is broadly accessible, so a genuine absence of any self-directed creative work is more likely to
  be a real engagement gap than an access-explained one, relative to costlier evidence types
  elsewhere in this package. Confidence: medium (reasoned distinction; material/software cost
  floors do vary by specific design sub-field, e.g. digital/product design may have a higher
  software-access bar than sketch-based work).
- `RULE-COUNSEL-086` — İŞKUR's official Future Occupations list includes Mimar (Architect), İç
  Mimar (Interior Architect), Tasarım (Design), and Modelist (Fashion/Pattern Designer),
  confirming domestic Turkish labor-market relevance for career-exploration framing only.
  Confidence: high (official source, for the list's content).
