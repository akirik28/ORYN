# Major-Family Evidence: Literature, Journalism & Communication

Scope: creative writing/literature, journalism, media/communication studies.

## Journalism specifically: published work is the evidence, and quality beats quantity explicitly

University journalism-program admissions guidance is unusually explicit and directly quotable on
this family's evidence standard: portfolios should show a student's **best published work**, not
"a collection of mediocre clips... thrown together to meet a requirement" — sources recommend
**three to five** strong clips over a longer list, explicitly framing careful selection itself as a
skill ("shows you have an editing eye"). Guidance also distinguishes **hard-news reporting** from
features/opinion/reviews as the more differentiating evidence, since "newspapers and magazines
rarely assign beginners primarily to features, columns, or reviews" professionally. (Source:
university journalism-program admissions/portfolio pages — high confidence for program-level
guidance, describing undergraduate/professional admission specifically; adapted below for
high-school stage.) [[RULE-COUNSEL-046]] This is one of the cleanest real-world confirmations in
this whole research package of the general "quality over quantity" finding from
`01-development-taxonomy.md` §2 — stated by a field's own gatekeepers about their own field's
specific evidence type, not inferred by analogy.

**High-school-stage adaptation**: student/school newspapers, a personal blog with substantive
reporting or analysis (not journaling), or contributions to a local outlet are the realistic
evidence channel — "published in an established independent news organization" (the undergraduate-
level bar cited above) is a higher bar than most high schoolers can or need to meet; what carries
over is the *underlying standard* (reported, edited, substantive work beats a large volume of
unedited personal writing), not the specific publication-venue requirement.

## Literature/creative writing: an official, century-long-running evidence channel

The **Scholastic Art & Writing Awards** (since 1923, the longest-running program of its kind in the
US, ~330,000 submissions annually across ~29 categories including poetry, novel writing, journalism,
and more) judges submissions on **originality, technical skill, and personal voice**, with a
tiered result structure (Gold Key / Silver Key / Honorable Mention) that maps directly onto this
package's achievement-tier ladder. (Source: `scholastic.com` official program page — high
confidence.) [[RULE-COUNSEL-047]] Its scale (330,000+ annual submissions) is itself useful context
for calibration: like the research-publication and competition-placement figures elsewhere in this
package, most entrants do not medal — participation and submission remain real evidence of
sustained creative work even without a Key award.

## Mapping onto the shipped taxonomy

- `execution_project_depth` — published writing, a sustained blog/publication, a completed creative
  work (short story collection, a finished manuscript draft).
- `intellectual_curiosity` — wide reading, engagement with craft beyond assignments.
- `awards_distinction` — Scholastic Awards tiers, journalism awards, when present — not required.
- `career_exploration` — school-paper roles (reporter → editor progression is a clean, accessible
  leadership/escalation pathway per `05-redundancy-saturation.md`'s tier-progression model).

## Unsafe inferences specific to this family

- **Do not treat volume of personal writing (a large number of unpublished poems/journal entries)
  as equivalent evidence to a smaller set of edited, shared/published work** — directly per §1's
  sourced finding; the counseling implication is to encourage *finishing and sharing* fewer pieces
  over accumulating many unshared drafts, framed as a craft-development suggestion, not a
  judgment on the unshared work's worth.
- **Do not require professional/paid publication as a bar for high-school-stage evidence** — per
  the high-school-stage adaptation above.

## Rules established in this document

- `RULE-COUNSEL-046` — For journalism-track evidence, favor a small number of substantive,
  edited/reported pieces over a large volume of unedited writing; adapt the "published in an
  established outlet" undergraduate bar down to school/local-outlet-appropriate evidence for
  high-school-stage students. Confidence: high (official/program-level source, explicitly adapted
  for age-appropriateness by this package).
- `RULE-COUNSEL-047` — Treat the Scholastic Art & Writing Awards as a well-established, tiered,
  official evidence channel for creative writing specifically, calibrated against its own large
  applicant pool so non-medaling submission is still treated as real evidence of sustained craft.
  Confidence: high (official source).
