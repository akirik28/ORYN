# Major-Family Evidence: Social Work

**Added after this session's original 8-family scope was complete**, found during a
coverage cross-check against the mission's own "expand more fields" continuation instruction.
Distinct from both `11-psychology-behavioral-science.md` (clinical/assessment/research-oriented)
and generic `community_impact`-dimension volunteering (episodic, not career-track): social work is
its own licensed profession with its own degree pathway, practicum structure, and — as this
document's country notes below show — a genuinely three-way-comparable licensure story similar in
shape to the peer session's `18-education-teaching.md`.

## 1. Field identity and subfields

Social work spans **clinical/direct-practice** work (individual/family casework, often the most
visible face of the profession), **community/macro practice** (program design, policy, community
organizing), **medical/healthcare social work** (embedded in hospitals/clinics), **school social
work**, and **child/family welfare** (protective services, foster care systems) — genuinely
different day-to-day work sharing a common credentialing foundation. [[RULE-COUNSEL-094]] The
same umbrella-field caution this package applies elsewhere (computing, engineering, teaching):
"wanting to help people" or "interested in social work" is not one undifferentiated interest.

## 2. Core skills

Per BLS's occupational profile: social workers "help people cope with challenges in their lives,"
requiring strong interpersonal/communication skill, organizational skill (caseload management),
and — a genuinely load-bearing skill for this field specifically — emotional resilience, since the
work routinely involves engaging with clients in crisis. (Source: BLS Occupational Outlook
Handbook, Social Workers — official, high confidence.) [[RULE-COUNSEL-095]] Beyond BLS: case
documentation/writing, cross-agency coordination (working with schools, healthcare providers,
courts, other social services), and cultural competence given the field's direct engagement with
diverse populations.

## 3. Career families

Direct clinical/casework roles (child and family social workers, healthcare social workers, mental
health/substance-abuse social workers — BLS tracks these as related but distinct occupational
categories); **licensed clinical social work specifically** requires the most credentialing (an
MSW from a CSWE-accredited program plus supervised post-graduate clinical hours, per §6) and
authorizes independent clinical practice/diagnosis, distinct from a bachelor's-level social work
role. Beyond direct practice: social work administration/program management, policy/advocacy work
(a natural link to `12-political-science-ir-public-policy.md`), and social work education/research.
**Counter-stereotypical example**: many people doing genuinely social-work-shaped work (case
management, community program coordination) hold adjacent degrees (psychology, sociology, public
health) rather than a formal social work degree — the *licensed clinical* track specifically is
what requires the CSWE-accredited MSW pathway; broader "social services" work is more permeable.

## 4. Exploration pathways by stage

**Phase 1:** volunteering with a service organization serving a population the student is drawn to
(youth, elderly, homelessness, disability services) — genuine, accessible entry evidence, and
exactly the kind of activity that would otherwise only show up generically under
`community_impact`; this family's exploration pathway *is* what gives that volunteering a specific
professional-direction reading rather than a generic-service reading.

**Phase 2:** sustained (not one-off) volunteering or a structured youth-facing program specifically
oriented toward direct service/casework-adjacent work (peer support programs, hotline/crisis-line
youth volunteer programs where age-appropriate, structured mentorship of at-risk peers) — the
family's analogue to `01-development-taxonomy.md`'s `career_exploration` construct, testing
whether a student is drawn to the actual relational/casework activity, not only the cause.
[[RULE-COUNSEL-096]]

## 5. Interdisciplinary connections

**Social work + psychology**: the two fields' undergraduate preparation overlaps substantially
(psychology coursework is common preparation for an MSW), but the *professional* tracks diverge —
see `11-psychology-behavioral-science.md` for the clinical-psychology comparison; the key
distinction for a counselor to surface is assessment/research/therapy-modality focus (psychology)
vs. case-management/systems-navigation/advocacy focus (social work). **Social work + public
policy**: macro-practice social work and policy work overlap heavily — see
`12-political-science-ir-public-policy.md`. **Social work + education**: school social work sits
directly between this family and the peer session's `18-education-teaching.md`.

## 6. Professional/licensure notes — a third clean three-country comparison

**United States**: licensed *clinical* social work requires an MSW from a **CSWE-accredited**
program plus supervised post-graduate clinical experience (commonly cited around 3,000 hours over
2+ years, state-dependent) and a clinical licensing exam; all states require licensure for clinical
practice, with specifics varying by state. (Source: BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook, Social
Workers, plus corroborating licensing-guide sources — high confidence for the BLS-sourced
structure, medium for the specific hour figure, which is described as commonly-cited rather than
federally fixed.) [[RULE-COUNSEL-097]]

**United Kingdom**: practising as a social worker requires **registration with Social Work
England** (or the equivalent body in Scotland/Wales/Northern Ireland) after completing an
approved undergraduate or postgraduate social work qualification (3-4 years full-time
undergraduate, or a postgraduate route for those with a prior degree) — a title-protection
structure (the professional title itself is legally regulated) directly analogous to
`18-education-teaching.md`'s QTS finding and `16-architecture-design.md`'s "architect" finding.
(Source: UK social-work-careers guidance including NHS Health Careers and official-guidance-
adjacent sources — medium-high confidence; Social Work England's own registration page was not
independently fetched this pass.) [[RULE-COUNSEL-098]]

**Turkey**: **upgraded to primary-source confirmation on a follow-up pass** — the peer session
independently fetched the actual regulation text (MEB's "Rehberlik Hizmetleri Yönetmeliği," Resmî
Gazete/Official Gazette, 10 Kasım 2017, Sayı 30236) via a direct PDF read (the same technique this
document's own Turkey career-outlook note used for İŞKUR's list, after WebFetch failed on the
official portal the same way it failed for this session on several occasions tonight). **Article
3(p) of that regulation defines "Sosyal Çalışmacı" verbatim as requiring "sosyal hizmet alanında
lisans eğitimi"** (bachelor's-level education specifically in the field of social work/social
services) — confirming this document's core claim exactly, not as a paraphrase. [[RULE-COUNSEL-099]]

**One part of the original claim could not be confirmed in this specific regulation, and is
downgraded accordingly rather than left at its original confidence**: the claim that associate-
degree ("ön lisans") graduates specifically receive different titles ("sosyal yardımcı," "sosyal
tekniker") is **not addressed, positively or negatively, in this particular regulation** — this
document (which governs guidance/counseling-center staff specifically, not all social-work
employment generally) only positively defines what a bachelor's-degree holder is called; it is
silent on associate-degree titling. That distinction may hold under a different regulation
governing social-work employment more broadly, but this document should not claim primary-source
confirmation for it — **treated as secondary-sourced only, not verified**, pending someone
checking the right regulation. [[RULE-COUNSEL-107]] (Source: MEB Rehberlik Hizmetleri Yönetmeliği,
official Turkish government regulation, fetched directly by the peer session and reported to this
session — high confidence for Article 3(p)'s bachelor's-degree definition specifically; the
associate-degree-titling claim remains at its original, weaker secondary-source confidence, now
explicitly flagged as unconfirmed by the one primary source actually checked, per this correction.)

**Cross-country pattern, matching `18-education-teaching.md`'s own §6 finding almost exactly**: all
three systems converge on a **two-part structure** (a relevant degree at a specific level, plus a
separate registration/licensure step controlling who may use the professional title) even though
mechanics differ (US: MSW + supervised hours + state license; UK: approved qualification + Social
Work England registration; Turkey: bachelor's-specifically + MEB-defined title). Worth stating to a
student as the general pattern, while being explicit the exact mechanics are country-specific.
[[RULE-COUNSEL-100]]

## Rules established in this document

- `RULE-COUNSEL-094` — Treat "social work" as spanning genuinely distinct subfields (clinical,
  macro/community, medical, school, child welfare); probe which before assuming evidence
  relevance. Confidence: high (structural, consistent with this package's umbrella-field pattern).
- `RULE-COUNSEL-095` — Recognize emotional resilience as a load-bearing, not secondary, skill for
  this family specifically, per BLS's own framing. Confidence: high (official source).
- `RULE-COUNSEL-096` — Distinguish sustained, casework-adjacent volunteering from generic episodic
  volunteering when evaluating social-work-track evidence — the former tests fit with the actual
  professional activity, the latter only tests general service orientation. Confidence:
  medium-high (reasoned, consistent with this package's general "subject enthusiasm ≠ the
  activity itself" pattern).
- `RULE-COUNSEL-097` — US clinical social work licensure requires a CSWE-accredited MSW plus
  supervised post-graduate clinical hours and a licensing exam, state-specific in details.
  Confidence: high (official BLS source for structure), medium (secondary-sourced for the
  specific hour figure).
- `RULE-COUNSEL-098` — UK social work practice requires Social Work England registration (or
  devolved-nation equivalent) after an approved qualification — a title-protected profession like
  UK teaching and architecture. Confidence: medium-high (not independently fetched from Social
  Work England's own page this pass).
- `RULE-COUNSEL-099` — Turkey's "sosyal çalışmacı" title is officially bachelor's-degree-specific
  — confirmed via the primary regulation text itself (MEB Rehberlik Hizmetleri Yönetmeliği,
  Art. 3(p), fetched directly by the peer session). Confidence: high, upgraded from secondary-
  source on a follow-up pass.
- `RULE-COUNSEL-107` — The claim that associate-degree graduates receive a different, narrower
  title ("sosyal yardımcı"/"sosyal tekniker") is **not confirmed** by the one primary regulation
  checked (which is silent on associate-degree titling, governing guidance-center staff
  specifically) — downgraded to secondary-source-only confidence rather than left at its original
  higher rating once the gap was found. A concrete example of this package's own discipline
  applied to itself: a partial primary-source confirmation should sharpen *and* narrow a claim,
  not be treated as blanket validation of everything originally asserted alongside it. Confidence:
  low-medium (the underlying claim may still be true under a different regulation, simply not
  verified by what was actually checked).
- `RULE-COUNSEL-100` — State the general "degree + separate registration/title-control step"
  pattern to students while being explicit that exact mechanics are country-specific — directly
  mirrors `18-education-teaching.md`'s own §6 finding for a different profession, suggesting this
  two-part structure may be a broader pattern across regulated-title professions this package
  should watch for in any future family additions. Confidence: medium (two data points, a
  plausible pattern, not yet a broadly-tested rule).
