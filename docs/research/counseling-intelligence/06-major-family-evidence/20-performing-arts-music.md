# 06.20 — Performing Arts & Music

**Added after the original 17 (and family 18) were complete** — found by checking the peer
session's `17-visual-media-arts.md` for performing-arts coverage specifically (given "Visual &
Media Arts" as a family name) and confirming it covers only visual arts and film, with no
treatment of music, theater, or dance anywhere in either session's families. A genuinely common,
career-relevant field for this age group, with real product-side signal it matters: ORYN's own
`lib/vocabularies/skills.ts` already lists Piano, Guitar, Violin, and Music Production among its
skill suggestions. No onboarding-suggestion anchor exists (the same real gap already noted for
family 18) — nearest matches are "Design" (imperfectly) or a custom/free-text interest.

## 1. Field identity and subfields

Splits along two axes: **discipline** (music — performance, composition, music education;
theater/acting; dance) and, within music specifically, **genre/tradition** (classical/conservatory-
track vs. contemporary/commercial). **RULE-COUNSEL-237 (new):** these are meaningfully different
day-to-day activities and, as this document's §6 shows, sometimes genuinely different admission
mechanisms (a classical-track conservatory audition is a different process from a general
performing-arts degree audition) — probe which a student actually means before treating "music" or
"performing arts" as one interest.

## 2. Core skills

Per BLS: musicians/singers "need extensive training and regular practice to acquire their skills,"
and — notably, unlike most other families in this package — **formal postsecondary education is
explicitly not required to enter the occupation** for many genres, though classical/opera
specifically often involves a bachelor's or higher degree [S-BLS-PERFORM]. Dancers "begin training
at a young age" and require "many years of formal training" regardless of eventual genre
[S-BLS-PERFORM] — this family's core skill-development timeline starts earlier, relative to
graduation, than almost any other family in this package, a genuinely distinctive finding worth
cross-referencing against `03-recommendation-timing.md`'s stage model (early, sustained
specialization is normal and expected here, not a sign of premature narrowing the way it might be
read for other families at the same age).

**A gap in this document's own first draft, found on re-reading against the peer's health/
disability-access work rather than caught the first time:** BLS states plainly that "dancers have
one of the highest rates of injuries and illnesses of all occupations" [S-BLS-PERFORM] — this
family has a genuine, documented physical-demand dimension that a student with a physical
disability, chronic illness, or injury history intersects with directly, and this document said
nothing about it in its first version. **RULE-COUNSEL-240 (new):** never assume this family's
performance-track pathways (dance specifically, but physically-demanding instrumental practice too)
are equally accessible to every student without a stated need — and, just as importantly, **never
assume the reverse**, that a disability precludes this family: adaptive dance, wheelchair-inclusive
performance, and disability arts more broadly are real, growing practice areas this document did
not research in depth (out of scope to do justice to in this pass) but must not be inferred as
nonexistent by omission. This is the same general "access/context" caution the peer session's
`04-profile-gap-framework.md` establishes generally — this document's contribution is naming the
one concrete, sourced, family-specific data point (BLS's own injury-rate statement) that makes the
general caution non-abstract for this particular family.

## 3. Career families

Beyond performing itself: composition/music direction (per BLS's dedicated Music Directors and
Composers profile [S-BLS-PERFORM]), choreography and dance direction (a common non-performing
continuation once "many dancers stop performing by the time they reach their late thirties because
of the physical demands of their work" [S-BLS-PERFORM] — a genuinely distinctive career-longevity
consideration this package has not encountered in any other family), teaching/private instruction
(a direct link to family 18, Education & Teaching), arts administration, and music
therapy (a licensed clinical field in some countries, distinct from performance — flagged, not
elaborated in depth here). **Counter-stereotypical example:** many working musicians hold day jobs
or parallel careers entirely outside music — BLS's own median-hourly-wage framing for musicians/
singers ($42.45/hour, May 2024 [S-BLS-PERFORM]) implicitly reflects a field with substantial
freelance/gig-based, non-continuous employment, not a single "musician" job category the way
`RULE-COUNSEL-058`/`059` (never equate popularity/growth-rate with individual fit) already cautions
against overreading for any family.

## 4. Exploration pathways by stage

**Phase 1:** general lessons/instruction (already common at this age for this family specifically,
per §2's early-start finding), school ensemble/theater participation.

**Phase 2:** competitive/juried performance opportunities are this family's equivalent of an
academic olympiad, and — like the chemistry/biology olympiads in families 03/04 — provide a
genuine, third-party-verified evidence point distinct from self-reported skill. The **Kennedy
Center's National Symphony Orchestra Young Soloists Competition** and **Carnegie Hall's National
Youth Orchestra of the United States (NYO-USA)** are both official, highly selective, prestigious
national programs run by major arts institutions [S-YOUTH-MUSIC] — genuinely comparable in
evidentiary weight to this package's other family-specific national competitions (e.g. the
US Chemistry Olympiad in family 03). Beyond competition: a sustained portfolio of recorded/performed
work (directly the `execution_project_depth` construct from `01-development-taxonomy.md`), and,
distinctively for this family, a formal audition itself — since many conservatory/performing-arts
programs weight a live or recorded audition above (or alongside) academic credentials (§6).

## 6. Professional/licensure notes — and a genuinely distinctive admission mechanism worth naming explicitly

**No general personal licensure** applies to performers themselves in any of the three countries
checked (unlike medicine, law, teaching, or engineering elsewhere in this package) — the closest
analogue is **professional body membership** rather than a legal practice requirement: in the UK,
**Equity** is named as the actors' professional body, providing job-finding/training support, with
membership requiring having already earned money from acting work or being a current student/
graduate [S-NCS-PERFORM] — a chicken-and-egg professional-recognition structure distinct from every
licensure system elsewhere in this package (you need paid work to join the body that helps you find
paid work), worth naming to a student rather than assuming a clean credential ladder like
teaching's or engineering's.

**United Kingdom:** National Careers Service's own profiles for dancer, actor, and musician
converge on a genuinely distinctive pattern for this family specifically: **"academic
qualifications may not be essential if you show enough talent at audition"** for dancers
specifically [S-NCS-PERFORM] — a direct, official statement that this family's admission/entry
logic can run on demonstrated talent *instead of* credentials, unlike every other family in this
package where a credential of some kind (degree, license, certification) is close to universal.

**Turkey (verified directly against official conservatory exam guidance, not secondhand):** Turkish
**state conservatories (Devlet Konservatuvarı)** admit via a dedicated **özel yetenek sınavı**
(special talent exam) — a genuinely hybrid mechanism distinct from Turkey's general YKS-only
system described in `03-recommendation-timing.md` §6: candidates must clear a **minimum TYT score
threshold** (150 points for most music/performing-arts programs, 180 for composition/theater/opera/
musicology specifically, per the guidance checked) *and* pass a talent exam (instrument performance,
technique, musicality, intonation, plus an interview stage, with non-written stages recorded audio/
video) [S-CONSERVATORY-TR]. **RULE-COUNSEL-238 (new):** this is a real, important refinement to
this package's own YKS-caveat framework — conservatory/performing-arts admission in Turkey is
**not** purely academic-exam-driven the way `RULE-COUNSEL-228` describes for the general YKS system;
it is a hybrid of a (lower) academic threshold plus an audition, structurally closer to the UK's
"talent can substitute for/supplement credentials" pattern than to YKS's pure-score placement. A
Turkey-target student interested specifically in this family should not have the blanket YKS
caveat applied without this refinement.
