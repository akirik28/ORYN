# 06.02 — Mathematics & Statistics

Covers the mission's "mathematics" and "statistics" raw fields, plus actuarial science as a
career-family destination (not a separate major in most systems). Onboarding anchor:
**Mathematics**.

## 1. Field identity and subfields

Pure mathematics (proof-based, structure-focused: algebra, analysis, topology, number theory) and
applied mathematics (mathematics in service of a real-world modeling problem: differential
equations, mathematical physics, financial mathematics) are a real internal split, alongside
statistics (data-focused: probability theory, inference, experimental design) which is
increasingly taught as its own track distinct from a general math major, especially at the
graduate level. **RULE-COUNSEL-039:** a student who says "I like math" may mean anything from
"I enjoy proof and abstraction" to "I like using formulas to solve applied problems" to "I like
analyzing data" — these predict different downstream fits (pure research vs. engineering/
quant-finance vs. data science) and should not be treated as one undifferentiated signal, the same
principle as `01-computing-information-sciences.md` §1's CS-umbrella caution.

## 2. Core skills

Per BLS's Occupational Outlook Handbook for Mathematicians and Statisticians: "computer programming
skills will remain important to many employers," staying current with new statistical methods, and
—matching a pattern already seen in the computing family — **strong communication skills to
interpret and present findings** are named explicitly as differentiating job candidates, not merely
technical skill in isolation [S-BLS-MATHSTAT]. For actuaries specifically, BLS names "a strong
background in mathematics, statistics, and business," analytical pattern-recognition in complex
data, and programming/spreadsheet/database tooling [S-BLS-ACTUARY]. Beyond BLS: proof-writing and
abstract reasoning (pure track), numerical computation and modeling tools (applied track),
statistical software and experimental design (statistics track).

## 3. Career families

Beyond "becomes a mathematician" (a genuinely small fraction of math graduates, mostly those
pursuing doctoral research and academia): **actuarial science** is a clean, well-documented
counter-stereotypical example of major≠career — actuaries are credentialed not by a specific
university degree but by passing a sequence of professional exams administered by bodies like the
Society of Actuaries (SOA) and Casualty Actuarial Society (CAS), with university coursework
(commonly statistics, economics, finance, accounting) earning exam-credit ("VEE," Validation by
Educational Experience) rather than being a mandatory prerequisite major [S-SOA-PATH]. BLS projects
actuarial employment growing 22% 2024–2034, "much faster than average" [S-BLS-ACTUARY] — cited here
as a real, sourced US data point, not a claim about any individual student's prospects (per this
package's own non-negotiable against implying individual probability from aggregate statistics).
Other career families: quantitative finance/trading (see `07-economics-finance.md`), data science
(see `01-computing-information-sciences.md`), operations research, cryptography/security, and
teaching/academia (the "becomes a mathematician" path, real but numerically small).

## 4. Exploration pathways by stage

**Phase 1:** math competitions at an accessible level (school/regional rounds of AMC-style
contests), an accessible applied project (e.g., modeling a simple real-world system), general
statistics exposure (a school project analyzing real data).

**Phase 2:** deeper competition involvement (national/international olympiad tracks) *specifically*
demonstrates proof-based/abstract problem-solving speed under contest conditions — a real and
valuable signal for pure-math-track fit, but **should not be treated as equivalent evidence for
applied-math or statistics fit**, which are better evidenced by a modeling or data-analysis project
with a real dataset or system. An actuarial-exam attempt (the first preliminary SOA/CAS exam is
open to students, not gated behind a completed degree) is a distinctive, verifiable, and
under-used piece of evidence for a student seriously considering that specific career family —
worth naming explicitly to students who express interest in it, since it is one of the few
pre-university opportunities in this whole family with an unambiguous, third-party-administered
outcome.

## 5. Interdisciplinary connections

**Math + economics** (quantitative finance, econometrics): see `07-economics-finance.md` for the
reciprocal framing — a joint project (e.g., statistical analysis of real economic data) is stronger
combined evidence than either alone for a student expressing both interests. **Math + CS**
(theoretical computer science, cryptography, algorithms): see `01-computing-information-sciences.md`.
**Math + physics** (mathematical/theoretical physics): see `03-physical-sciences.md`.

## 6. Professional/licensure notes

**No licensure requirement** for mathematician/statistician roles generally. **Actuarial work is
the one genuine credential-gated career family in this cluster** — not licensure in the
medicine/law/engineering sense (no government-issued license is required to be employed as an
actuarial analyst), but professional recognition (ASA/FSA designations, or CAS's equivalent) requires
passing a defined exam sequence over several years, administered by the SOA/CAS specifically for
the US/Canada system [S-SOA-PATH]. UK/European actuarial credentialing runs through a related but
distinct body (the Institute and Faculty of Actuaries in the UK) with its own exam structure — **not
independently verified in this research pass**; flagged in `10-open-questions.md` rather than
assumed equivalent to the US/Canada SOA/CAS structure.
