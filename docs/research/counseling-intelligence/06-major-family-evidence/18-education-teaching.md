# 06.18 — Education & Teaching

**Added after both sessions' original 17-family taxonomy was complete** — found missing during a
cross-check of the whole package for genuine coverage gaps (per the mission's own "expand more
fields" continuation instruction). Teaching/education appeared only as a passing "could go into
academia/teaching" mention within the peer session's psychology and sociology/history/philosophy
family docs, with no dedicated treatment anywhere across either session's 17 families — a real gap
given how common and globally relevant this career path is for ORYN's target age group. No
dedicated onboarding-suggestion anchor exists for this interest either (a real gap in its own
right, consistent with `00-family-taxonomy.md` §4's existing note about onboarding-vocabulary
gaps) — nearest matches are a custom/free-text interest, or overlap with a student's chosen
subject-matter interest (e.g. "I want to teach math" spans this family and `02-mathematics-
statistics.md`).

## 1. Field identity and subfields

Teaching splits meaningfully by **level** (early childhood/elementary, middle/secondary, post-
secondary) and by **specialization** (subject-specific at the secondary level; generalist at the
elementary level; special education as a distinct, additionally-credentialed specialization).
**RULE-COUNSEL-232 (new):** "wanting to teach" is not one undifferentiated interest — a student
drawn to elementary generalist teaching, secondary subject-specialist teaching, and special
education are developing toward substantially different day-to-day work and, in most systems,
different credentialing tracks, the same umbrella-field caution already established for computing,
engineering, and other families in this package.

## 2. Core skills

Per BLS's occupational profiles for kindergarten/elementary and high school teachers: subject-
matter competence (particularly pronounced at the secondary level, where teachers "generally
specialize in a subject, such as English, math, or science" [S-BLS-TEACH]), plus — a pattern that,
unlike every other family surveyed in this package, is not merely a secondary skill here but
teaching's **entire core competency** — instructional communication: preparing academic lessons,
explaining material to a range of learners, and (per the high-school-specific profile) preparing
students for both higher education and the workforce [S-BLS-TEACH]. Classroom management,
assessment design, and — increasingly, per the same sources — social-emotional awareness (special
education teachers in particular) round out the field's core skill set.

## 3. Career families

Per BLS: kindergarten/elementary teaching is a large occupational category with modest projected
growth (~1% through 2032 per the fetched snapshot) but substantial absolute openings (~109,000/year,
driven mostly by replacement rather than growth) [S-BLS-TEACH]; median pay for elementary teachers
was $62,340 (May 2024) [S-BLS-TEACH]. Beyond classroom teaching itself: instructional design/
curriculum development, school counseling (a distinct credential in most systems, not a default
teaching-career extension), educational administration (principal/school-leadership roles,
typically requiring both teaching experience and additional administrative credentialing), and
education policy/research (a natural link to the peer session's `12-political-science-ir-public-
policy.md`). **Counter-stereotypical example:** a meaningful share of people who teach, especially
at the university/adult-education level, hold degrees in their taught subject (e.g. a physics
degree, a literature degree) rather than an education-specific degree — teaching is one of this
package's clearest cases where the "major" that leads to a teaching career is often the *subject
matter* degree plus a separate, additional pedagogical credential, not a single unified "education
major" pipeline.

## 4. Exploration pathways by stage

**Phase 1:** informal teaching/tutoring of younger students or peers (a common, highly accessible
entry point unique to this family — nearly every student has *some* opportunity to explain
something to someone younger, unlike more specialized fields), general coursework engagement in a
subject a student might want to teach.

**Phase 2:** sustained, structured tutoring or teaching-assistant roles (school-organized peer-
tutoring programs, structured after-school teaching of younger students) — this is the family's
version of `01-development-taxonomy.md`'s `career_exploration` construct, directly testing whether
a student enjoys the actual instructional act, not just the subject matter itself. **RULE-COUNSEL-
233 (new):** sustained, structured tutoring/instruction of others is meaningfully stronger evidence
for this family than subject-matter achievement alone (e.g., being good at math is not the same
evidence as enjoying and being effective at *teaching* math) — a real, family-specific instance of
the general "subject enthusiasm ≠ the actual professional activity" caution this package raises
elsewhere (e.g. `06-major-family-evidence/07-economics-finance.md` §1's analogous caution for
economics).

## 5. Interdisciplinary connections

**Education + any subject-matter family** (a defining feature of this family, not a single
combination to name): a student's "what to teach" question routes directly to whichever of this
package's other 17 families matches their subject interest — this family covers the pedagogical/
instructional dimension common across all of them. **Education + psychology** (educational
psychology, learning science): see the peer session's `11-psychology-behavioral-science.md`.
**Education + policy** (education policy, school administration): see the peer session's
`12-political-science-ir-public-policy.md`. **Education + special education/disability studies**:
a distinct enough specialization to warrant its own additional credentialing in most systems
checked (see §6) — not elaborated as a full sub-family here, flagged for `10-open-questions.md`
as a possible future deepening.

## 6. Professional/licensure notes — genuinely important, and country-specific in an unusually clean, three-way-comparable way

**United States:** public-school teaching requires a **state-issued certification or license**
(BLS states this plainly for both elementary and high-school teachers [S-BLS-TEACH]), typically
requiring a bachelor's degree, a teacher-preparation program, and passing a certification exam
(commonly a Praxis exam) [S-TEACH-CERT-US]. **Licensure is genuinely state-specific** — requirements,
including whether a master's degree is required within a set period after initial licensure, vary
by state, more so than the federally-harmonized professional structures seen in some other
families [S-TEACH-CERT-US].

**United Kingdom:** the analogous credential is **Qualified Teacher Status (QTS)**, awarded by the
Department for Education and legally required to teach in most state-maintained schools in England
[S-QTS-UK]. Multiple official routes exist (undergraduate initial teacher training; postgraduate
routes including the PGCE or School Direct for those who already hold a degree), with a GCSE
Maths/English (or equivalent) requirement and, in England specifically, professional skills tests
[S-QTS-UK]. Newly-qualified teachers must additionally complete a **two-year Early Career Teacher
(ECT) induction period** after gaining QTS [S-QTS-UK] — a structured post-credential mentorship
period with no precise US equivalent found in this pass.

**Turkey:** verified directly against the Ministry of National Education's (MEB) own regulation —
teaching appointment requires graduating from a higher-education program relevant to the specific
teaching field, completing **pedagojik formasyon** (pedagogical formation training) where the
degree program itself doesn't already include it, and meeting a minimum score threshold on the
**KPSS** (the general civil-service exam used for first appointment to teaching posts, among other
public roles) for the specific field being applied to [S-MEB-TEACH]. **RULE-COUNSEL-234 (new):**
Turkey's teaching-credential system is presently changing — sourced directly, not secondhand: MEB
material indicates KPSS alone will no longer be sufficient, with a new requirement that candidate
teachers complete training at the **Milli Eğitim Akademisi** (National Education Academy) before
starting the role [S-MEB-TEACH]. A Turkey-target student should not be given a static, one-time
description of this pathway — this system is actively evolving and should be re-verified against
MEB's current guidance rather than treated as fixed.

**RULE-COUNSEL-235 (new):** all three systems converge on the same underlying **two-part
structure** (a relevant degree, plus a separate pedagogical/professional credentialing step) even
though the specific mechanics differ substantially (US: state license + exam; UK: QTS + ECT
induction; Turkey: pedagogical formation + KPSS, moving toward a National Education Academy
model) — worth stating to a student as the general pattern ("a subject degree alone is rarely
enough anywhere; expect a separate teaching-specific credentialing step"), while being explicit
that the *exact* mechanics are genuinely country-specific and must not be conflated across systems.

**A distinct mechanism worth naming separately, found via a cross-cutting lead from the peer
session's medicine-family research (RULE-COUNSEL-120: a third assessment type, standardized
situational-judgment testing, expanding beyond medicine into education):** the **Casper test**
(Acuity Insights) is a timed, open-response situational-judgment assessment now used for
**admission into teacher-education programs themselves** — a different gate than §6's licensure
discussion above, which governs certification to practice *after* completing a program. Verified
directly against Acuity Insights' own "Teacher Education" solutions page: Casper for this track is
framed as helping a program "identify motivated and resilient students" and select "applicants with
the highest potential to thrive in their program, and as future teachers," scored by trained human
raters (not AI) rather than multiple-choice. Confirmed institutional adoption for teacher-education
specifically at **Vancouver Island University** (B.Ed., Years 1 and 3) and (per Monash and Brock
University's own program pages, both independently surfaced) at least two further institutions —
**all confirmed examples found this pass are Canadian/Australian**; no US teacher-education program
was independently confirmed to require Casper in this pass, unlike its documented ~50-medical-school
US footprint (peer's `10-medicine-clinical-pathways.md`/RULE-COUNSEL-120). **RULE-COUNSEL-243
(new):** do not assume Casper (or a Casper-style situational-judgment test) applies to a US-target
teaching-track student by default — treat it as a real, confirmed, but currently non-US-demonstrated
admissions mechanism for this family, re-verify per-institution rather than assuming universally.
This is the same "rehearse a structured response, not log an achievement" recommendation-type gap
the peer session named in `10-open-questions.md` (RULE-COUNSEL-118) — ORYN's counselor has no
recommendation vocabulary for "practice a timed situational-judgment response" the way it does for
building a portfolio or logging an activity, and this family is now a second, confirmed place (after
Queen's Commerce/McMaster HHSP) where that gap is real, not hypothetical.
