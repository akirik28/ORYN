# 06.07 — Economics & Finance

Covers the mission's "economics" and "finance" raw fields. Onboarding anchor: **Economics**
(finance is not a separate onboarding suggestion but is covered here as a closely related career-
family destination).

## 1. Field identity and subfields

Economics splits broadly into micro/macroeconomic theory, econometrics/applied empirical work, and
policy-oriented economics; finance (often a separate major, especially in business-school contexts)
focuses more specifically on corporate finance, investments, and markets. **RULE-COUNSEL-046:**
economics is a genuinely quantitative social science — a student drawn to it purely for its
real-world relevance (news, policy debates) without any appetite for the statistics/econometrics
that underpin serious study of it is a real, worth-surfacing mismatch signal, distinct from (and
milder than) an outright unsafe inference — this is an observation worth a counselor conversation,
not a disqualification.

## 2. Core skills

Per BLS: economists conduct research, prepare reports, and evaluate monetary/fiscal policy issues,
collecting and analyzing statistical data as a core activity [S-BLS-ECON]; financial analysts need
strong analytical skill to process information in search of profitable investment decisions, and BLS
notes a master's degree can improve advancement prospects specifically for more senior analyst roles
(not a claim that a master's is required to enter the field) [S-BLS-FINANCE]. Beyond BLS: statistical/
econometric methods (directly overlapping `02-mathematics-statistics.md`'s statistics track),
data analysis, and — as the Federal Reserve's own youth-competition program states explicitly —
research, data literacy, critical thinking, and analytical writing as the actual skill bundle the
field rewards [S-FED-CHALLENGE], not merely "being good at math" in isolation.

## 3. Career families

Beyond "becomes an economist" (a research/policy-institution/central-bank/academic path, real but
not the majority destination for most economics graduates): financial analysis, investment
banking/asset management, corporate finance and strategy roles, policy analysis (government,
international organizations, think tanks), consulting, and — a well-documented pattern —
economics as a strong quantitative-generalist foundation feeding directly into data science and
technology roles, alongside its more expected finance destinations. **Professional certification as
a counter-stereotypical career-family marker:** the **Chartered Financial Analyst (CFA)**
designation, awarded by the CFA Institute, is a postgraduate professional credential earned through
a structured exam program rather than through a specific undergraduate major — economics, finance,
mathematics, engineering, and other quantitative majors all commonly feed into it [S-CFA] — another
concrete instance of the major≠career principle, structurally similar to the actuarial-credential
example in `02-mathematics-statistics.md` §3.

## 4. Exploration pathways by stage

**Phase 1:** general coursework engagement, an accessible personal-finance or basic-economics
project, following real economic events/policy debates with growing sophistication.

**Phase 2:** the **Federal Reserve System's own High School Fed Challenge** is a concrete, highly
authoritative exploration opportunity — a national team research-and-writing competition explicitly
open to grades 9-12 with **no prior economics coursework required** ("previous study of economics is
not required — only intellectual curiosity"), where teams research an annual real-world theme and
strong submissions are published in the Journal of Future Economists [S-FED-CHALLENGE]. This is
worth naming specifically to any economics-curious student, since it is (a) run directly by the
actual Federal Reserve System (about as official a source as this field has), (b) explicitly framed
as accessible to beginners, and (c) produces a genuine written-research output rather than only a
competition placement. The **National Economics Challenge** (Council for Economic Education) is a
comparable, separately-organized option [S-FED-CHALLENGE]. Beyond competitions: an independent
research project analyzing real economic data (directly usable as `research` dimension evidence per
`01-development-taxonomy.md` §2.4), or a sustained personal-investing/portfolio-tracking project
with genuine analytical reflection (not merely "I invested money" — the analysis and reasoning is
the evidence, not the account balance).

## 5. Interdisciplinary connections

**Economics + math/statistics** (econometrics, quantitative finance): see

## 6. Professional/licensure notes

**No general licensure requirement** for economist or financial-analyst roles as job titles.
Specific finance sub-roles carry their own regulatory requirements in the countries ORYN targets
(e.g., securities-industry roles in the US commonly require passing specific FINRA-administered
exams tied to the *employing firm's* sponsorship, not a personal license earned independently
in advance) — genuinely different in structure from the CFA credential (§3, a voluntary professional
distinction, not a legal practice requirement) or from PE/medical/legal licensure elsewhere in this
package. **Not exhaustively mapped in this research pass**; flagged in `10-open-questions.md`.

## 7. Country notes (UK / EU / Turkey)

**United Kingdom:** National Careers Service's official **Economist** profile lists a salary range
of roughly £28,000–£60,000 for a 37–39 hour week and describes the role as advising government
departments, businesses, and banks on economic matters [S-NCS-ECON]. Worth naming specifically: the
UK's **Government Economic Service (GES)** is a distinctive, named professional community of
economists working across UK government departments — a career family with no precise US analogue
at the same scale, since UK economic policy work is organized around this single cross-government
service rather than being distributed department-by-department the way it more commonly is in the
US federal system [S-GES]. The CFA credential (§3) is genuinely international and is pursued by UK-
based finance professionals at similar rates to US ones — not a US-only credential, unlike the Fed
Challenge competition.

**EU:** ESCO covers economics/finance occupations across EU languages (see
`01-computing-information-sciences.md` §7). No EU-wide licensure gate applies to economist roles;
specific finance-sector regulatory requirements (analogous to US FINRA) exist per-country/via EU
financial-services directives, not mapped in this pass.

**Turkey:** no field-specific official source independently verified this pass. Flagged in
`10-open-questions.md`.
