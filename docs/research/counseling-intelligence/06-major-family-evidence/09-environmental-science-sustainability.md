# 06.09 — Environmental Science & Sustainability

Covers the mission's "environmental science" and "sustainability" raw fields. Onboarding anchor:
**Environmental Science**.

## 1. Field identity and subfields

A genuinely interdisciplinary family by nature, not a single discipline: environmental *science*
(ecology, atmospheric/earth science, environmental chemistry — closer to the natural sciences,
see §5), environmental *engineering* (applying engineering to pollution control, water treatment,
sustainable infrastructure — closer to `05-engineering-me-ce-ae-ee.md`), conservation science
(wildlife/habitat/land management — a more field-based, less lab-based profile), and
sustainability/policy work (closer to `07-economics-finance.md` and the peer session's
`12-political-science-ir-public-policy.md`). **RULE-COUNSEL-215:** "environmental science" and
"sustainability" are not the same activity profile — a student drawn to fieldwork/conservation and
one drawn to sustainability policy/business practice are both legitimately in this family but
would benefit from different exploration experiences (§4), directly the same umbrella-field caution
already established for computing, biology, and engineering in earlier documents.

## 2. Core skills

Per BLS across this cluster: environmental engineers need interpersonal skill (coordinating with
scientists/technicians), applied mathematics (calculus, trigonometry, in analysis/design), and
problem-solving skill oriented specifically toward anticipating and preventing environmental harm
[S-BLS-ENVENG]; environmental scientists and specialists need interpersonal/team skill, analytical
skill grounded in careful data analysis, and communication skill for presenting findings "to
audiences of varying backgrounds" and writing technical reports [S-BLS-ENVSCI]; conservation
scientists and foresters specifically need management/leadership skill (directing conservation
crews) and, distinctively among every field surveyed in this research package, **physical
stamina** — BLS names walking long distances in difficult terrain and working in all weather
conditions as a real job requirement [S-BLS-CONSERVATION]. This last point is worth naming
explicitly to a student: this specific career family (conservation, distinct from environmental
engineering or policy) rewards a real comfort with sustained fieldwork, not only classroom/lab
aptitude.

## 3. Career families

Per BLS: environmental engineering growth is projected about average (4%, 2024-2034); conservation
science is expected to see demand growth driven by "heightened public interest" in habitat
protection and water-supply preservation specifically [S-BLS-CONSERVATION]. Beyond the
named-occupation roles: corporate sustainability roles (a fast-growing, less traditionally-defined
career family as more companies build dedicated sustainability functions — not yet as clearly
BLS-codified as the science/engineering roles above, so treated here as an observed trend rather
than a cited BLS category), environmental policy/regulatory roles, and environmental law (see the
peer session's `13-law-oriented-pathways.md`). **Major≠career note:** this family is unusually
receptive to students from *other* science/engineering majors who specialize into an environmental
application later (a chemist into environmental chemistry, a mechanical engineer into renewable-
energy systems) — environmental work is often a specialization layered onto another core science/
engineering major as much as it is its own dedicated major.

## 4. Exploration pathways by stage

**Phase 1:** an accessible citizen-science contribution (participating in an existing data-
collection effort — e.g., a biodiversity count, air/water quality monitoring), general coursework
engagement. Peer-reviewed research on youth-focused citizen science supports this as a genuine,
legitimate entry point specifically for building environmental science learning and agency, not
merely a busywork activity [S-CITIZEN-SCIENCE].

**Phase 2:** a sustained fieldwork or research placement — this family has a genuine pattern of
programs combining real conservation/field work (habitat restoration, invasive-species removal,
ecosystem monitoring) with mentorship, distinct from a pure lab-research profile; and programs
pairing a student with a scientist mentor on a specific applied environmental question (e.g., a
coastal or urban environmental issue) **are a well-documented category of opportunity in this
field specifically** [S-ENV-PROGRAMS-CATEGORY] — **named programs surfaced during this research
(a teen environmental research/mentorship program, a conservation-internship-placement
organization, a coastal-research mentorship program) are illustrative examples of this category,
sourced only from secondary aggregator discussion, not independently verified against each
program's own official page this pass — per this package's own source discipline, these should be
treated as discovery leads for ORYN's separate opportunity-acquisition pipeline to verify and
source properly, not asserted to a student as confirmed, current opportunities directly from this
document.**

## 5. Interdisciplinary connections

**Environmental science + engineering** (environmental/sustainable engineering, renewable energy):
see `05-engineering-me-ce-ae-ee.md` §5. **Environmental science + chemistry/physics**
(environmental chemistry, atmospheric science): see `03-physical-sciences.md`. **Environmental
science + biology** (ecology, conservation biology): see `04-life-sciences.md` §5.
**Sustainability + economics/policy** (environmental economics, climate policy): see
`07-economics-finance.md` and the peer session's `12-political-science-ir-public-policy.md`.

## 6. Professional/licensure notes

**No general personal licensure** for environmental scientist or conservation-scientist roles as
such. **Environmental engineering** inherits the same PE-licensure structure discussed in
`05-engineering-me-ce-ae-ee.md` §6 where an environmental engineer's work involves public
infrastructure/safety — not a separate licensure system, the same one. No further country-specific
verification performed for this family beyond what's already noted in the engineering document.

## 7. Country notes (UK / EU / Turkey)

**United Kingdom:** National Careers Service's official **Environmental Consultant** profile
(salary range roughly £25,000 starting to £50,000 experienced) explicitly names **Chartered
Environmentalist** status as a career-progression marker, alongside routes into running one's own
consultancy or moving into environmental research/education [S-NCS-ENVCON] — the same
"chartered status as career-long recognition, not entry gate" pattern already established for UK
chemistry/engineering elsewhere in this document set (`03-physical-sciences.md` §7,
`05-engineering-me-ce-ae-ee.md` §6). Entry routes include both university degrees and
apprenticeships, and accept related-but-not-identical subjects (biology, chemistry, geoscience,
ecology) alongside a dedicated environmental-science degree — directly corroborating this
document's own §3 "major≠career" note about this family being receptive to adjacent-science
entrants.

**EU:** ESCO covers environmental-science/engineering occupations across EU languages (see
`01-computing-information-sciences.md` §7). EU environmental regulation (a large, genuinely
distinct policy area — EU environmental law/directives) is out of scope for this document's own
depth; noted only as existing, not characterized further here.

**Turkey:** İŞKUR's official future-occupations list names **Çevre Mühendisi** (Environmental
Engineer) explicitly, plus the adjacent **Güneş Enerjisi Paneli Montaj Elemanı** (Solar Panel
Installation Technician) as a renewable-energy-specific role [S-ISKUR-FUTURE — see
`01-computing-information-sciences.md` §7]. "Environmental science" or "sustainability" as
standalone (non-engineering) named entries did not appear on that list. Flagged in
`10-open-questions.md`.
