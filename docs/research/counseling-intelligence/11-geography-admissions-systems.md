# Geography-Conditional Admissions Systems

Answers: *how much does holistic, activity-based profile-building actually matter for admission
across ORYN's explicit target geographies, and what should that mean for how the counselor weights
its recommendations by target country?*

This document did not exist in the original 10-document plan (`00-overview.md`). It was written
because `10-open-questions.md` §1 identified this as **the single highest-leverage gap** in the
core package: this whole research package's taxonomy, redundancy, and major-family findings are
grounded overwhelmingly in US-style holistic-admissions sources, while `AGENTS.md` §0 commits ORYN
to five target geographies (USA/UK/Europe/Turkey/international) and explicitly names France,
Netherlands, Germany, Italy, and Switzerland as priority European countries (§8). This document
researches those five plus firms up the USA/UK/Turkey findings already in `10`.

## The finding, stated as a three-tier framework

Every country's admissions system checked for this document falls cleanly into one of three tiers
by *how much a holistic activity/achievement profile actually affects the admission decision* —
not by how good or rigorous the system is, which is a different, value-laden question this
document does not attempt to answer.

### Tier 1 — Full holistic review (activity profile is a real, substantial input)

**United States** (already extensively sourced in `01`/`02`/`04`/`05`/`06`). The only system checked
where a rich, multi-dimensional activity profile is itself a primary, independently-weighted input
alongside academics — this is the system this whole package's core research was built against, and
it remains directly applicable here without modification.

### Tier 2 — Partial/subject-focused holistic review (some activity evidence matters, narrowly and unevenly)

- **United Kingdom (UCAS)** — already sourced in `10` §1 (`RULE-COUNSEL-058`). Confirmed here with
  more precision: the operative distinction is **"super-curricular" (subject-extending activity)**,
  weighted meaningfully for competitive courses, versus **general "extracurricular"** (leadership,
  sports, unrelated clubs), weighted lightly and "only when you can articulate the transferable
  skill." Predicted grades and the exam-based system (A-levels/IB) still carry substantial,
  separately-reported weight. Medicine is a named partial exception where structured work
  experience/volunteering is specifically expected, closer to a Tier-1 pattern for that one field.
- **France (Parcoursup)** — official government and Campus France sources describe the dossier
  each program receives: school report cards (bulletin), baccalauréat-track grades, a
  teacher/school-completed **"Fiche Avenir"** evaluation, and — for programs that request it — a
  motivation letter; "information about activities and interests" is also part of the file.
  Programs are explicitly described as weighting these elements differently depending on how
  selective ("sélectif") the specific program is — a highly selective *classe préparatoire* or
  competitive program leans more heavily on the full dossier including activities/motivation; a
  non-selective university license program leans close to automatic admission on grades/diploma
  alone. (Source: `campusfrance.org`, `education.gouv.fr` — official, high confidence for the
  mechanism; medium confidence for exactly how much weight activities carry relative to grades,
  which the sources describe as program-dependent rather than fixed.) [[RULE-COUNSEL-061]]
  **France is meaningfully more holistic than Germany/Netherlands/Switzerland/Italy below, but
  still grades-dominant and far lighter-touch than the US on activities specifically.**

### Tier 3 — Credential/score-gated (activity profile carries little to no formal weight)

- **Turkey (YKS)** — already sourced in `10` §1. Independently re-confirmed and deepened by the
  peer session (their `03` §6/`RULE-COUNSEL-062`, per this session's coordination log): a
  centralized exam score (TYT/AYT, and YDT for language-based programs) drives placement directly;
  **there is no application file, essay, or activity list submitted to Turkish universities via
  the standard YKS pathway at all** — admission is score-ranked placement, structurally different
  from Tier 1/2, not just less weighted.
- **Germany** — Abitur-grade-based; a **Numerus Clausus (NC)** grade cutoff (recalculated each
  admissions cycle from that cycle's actual applicant grades, not fixed in advance) gates the
  minority of high-demand programs, alongside waiting-period and small special-aptitude quotas
  ("Zusätzliche Eignungsquote," which can include tests or professional experience for a limited
  share of seats — the one place a non-grades factor formally enters). **A large share of programs
  are "zulassungsfrei" (officially: no admission restriction) — meeting the formal Abitur
  requirement grants a place with no selection process at all.** (Source: multiple
  admissions-guide sources for the NC mechanism, high confidence for the mechanism itself;
  official German university pages for the `zulassungsfrei` category's existence, confirming the
  category is real, medium confidence on what share of all programs nationally fall into it, which
  this package did not find a single consolidated statistic for.) [[RULE-COUNSEL-062]] No source
  checked names extracurricular activities as a general admissions factor for the standard
  domestic pathway.
- **Netherlands** — most programs are open-enrollment on the qualifying diploma; capacity-limited
  ("numerus fixus") programs use **decentralized selection** set independently by each university,
  which can include a motivation letter, CV, and sometimes a test/interview — a genuine soft-
  holistic element, but explicitly narrower than US practice: one source describes Dutch
  universities as generally not emphasizing extracurricular activities specifically, with academic
  performance remaining the key measure even within decentralized selection. (Source: university
  admissions-guide pages describing `numerus fixus`/`decentrale selectie` — medium-high confidence
  for the mechanism; medium confidence for the specific "don't emphasize extracurriculars" claim,
  sourced from a single describing source rather than official university policy pages directly.)
  [[RULE-COUNSEL-063]]
- **Italy** — bifurcated by institution type. Public universities in restricted fields (medicine
  being the clearest case) use a national standardized test (**IMAT** for the English-language
  track, an Italian-Ministry-designed test for the Italian track) — sourced guidance states plainly
  that for the IMAT-based public pathway "there is no need for top scoring high school grades,
  interviews, or other extracurricular activities." Private institutions, by contrast, more
  frequently request holistic portfolios including extracurricular/volunteer evidence. (Source:
  medicine-admissions-guidance sources — medium confidence, medicine-specific; this package did
  not verify whether the same public/private split holds as cleanly for non-medicine Italian
  programs.) [[RULE-COUNSEL-064]]
- **Switzerland** — the most extreme Tier-3 case found. A passing Swiss Matura (or recognized
  equivalent) grants admission to essentially all Swiss university programs with **no entrance
  exam, interview, or activity review at all** — explicitly true even for ETH Zürich and EPFL,
  Switzerland's most prestigious technical universities — with **medicine as the sole named
  exception**, gated instead by a separate centralized aptitude test (EMS), not by an activity
  portfolio. (Source: ETH Zürich official admissions pages, `maturite-suisse.ch` — high confidence,
  official source for ETH specifically; general-Swiss-system claim corroborated by secondary
  sources.) [[RULE-COUNSEL-065]]

### Synthesis table

| Country | Tier | Primary admission lever | Activity-profile weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| USA | 1 | Full holistic review | High, independently weighted |
| UK | 2 | Predicted grades + subject-relevant ("super-curricular") evidence | Moderate, narrowly subject-scoped; medicine partial exception toward Tier 1 |
| France | 2 (lighter than UK) | Grades/bac + program-dependent dossier | Low-moderate, varies sharply by program selectivity |
| Germany | 3 | Abitur grade (NC cutoff) or open admission | Minimal to none (small aptitude-quota exception) |
| Netherlands | 3 (soft exception for numerus fixus) | Diploma (open) or university-set decentralized selection | Low where present at all; absent for most programs |
| Italy | 3 (public) / 2 (private, uneven) | National test (restricted fields) or diploma | Minimal (public/IMAT); moderate (some private) |
| Switzerland | 3 (near-absolute) | Matura pass/fail | None (medicine: separate aptitude test, still not activity-based) |
| Turkey | 3 (most exam-only) | Centralized exam score (YKS) | None — no application file exists |

## What this means for ORYN's counselor — a proposed conditional layer (research only, not implemented)

This package proposes, without implementing, a weighting principle that should sit **above** the
9-dimension development taxonomy, conditioned on the student's stated target
geography/institutions (`target_universities`/`TargetGeography`, already collected per `AGENTS.md`
Phase 2/onboarding):

1. **The 9-dimension taxonomy remains valid as *development* guidance for every student,
   regardless of tier** — genuine intellectual engagement, depth over performative breadth, and
   honest evidence are good advice for a student's actual growth independent of any specific
   admissions system, and this package is not suggesting Tier-3-track students should stop being
   shown gap/opportunity guidance.
2. **What must change by tier is *how a recommendation is framed as mattering for admission*.**
   For a Tier-1 (US) or Tier-2-selective (UK/competitive-France) target, a recommendation can
   honestly say "this strengthens your application." For a Tier-3 target (Turkey/Germany-NC/
   Switzerland/most-Netherlands/Italy-public), the honest statement is closer to "this supports
   your development and may matter for [a specific named alternative pathway, e.g. a private
   institution, a scholarship, a non-standard admission route] — it is not what determines your
   [YKS/NC/Matura-pass/IMAT] admission outcome, which is driven by [exam score/grade]."
   [[RULE-COUNSEL-066]]
3. **For a Tier-3-track student, the highest-value recommendation category shifts toward academic/
   exam preparation specifically** — not because the other 8 dimensions stop mattering for the
   student as a person, but because they stop being the *admissions lever* ORYN's counselor should
   imply they are. A counselor that spent a Tier-3-track student's limited time nudging toward
   more `leadership`/`community_impact` evidence while their actual admission outcome is
   determined by an exam score would be optimizing the wrong variable for that student's *stated
   goal* (a specific admission outcome), even while giving generically reasonable development
   advice. [[RULE-COUNSEL-067]]
4. **This must be a per-student, per-target conditional, not a per-country default** — a Turkish
   student targeting a UK or US university (explicitly a normal case per ORYN's own "international
   students applying abroad" scope, `AGENTS.md` §0) needs Tier-1/2-appropriate counseling despite
   their `country` being Turkey; the conditional keys off **target institution/system**, not
   student nationality or residence. This is also a direct application of `08`'s (peer-owned)
   `RULE-COUNSEL-057` — never infer anything from a demographic/nationality signal — the same
   discipline applies here: a student's own *stated target*, never their country of origin, should
   drive this weighting. [[RULE-COUNSEL-068]]
5. **Mixed-target students (applying to both a Tier-1 and a Tier-3 system, a genuinely common real
   case for exactly ORYN's stated population)** need both kinds of guidance simultaneously,
   presented separately rather than blended into one undifferentiated recommendation set — blending
   them risks either over-weighting activities for the Tier-3 application or under-weighting exam
   prep for the Tier-1 one.

## Persona walkthrough — extending `09-persona-testing.md`'s Persona E

Persona E (`09`, Turkish/MEB curriculum, Economics goal, targeting "UK or Europe") is the natural
test case for finding 5 above: this persona's *country* is Turkey, but per §"what this means"
point 4, the operative variable is the *target* ("UK or Europe" — likely Tier 1/2, not Tier 3),
so **Persona E should receive UK/Europe-appropriate (activity-inclusive) counseling despite being
Turkish** — confirming Persona E's original walkthrough in `09` didn't get this wrong (it never
assumed Turkish nationality implied YKS-track counseling), but making explicit *why* that was the
right call, which `09` itself had not yet articulated since this document didn't exist yet.

**New persona for this document — Persona H**: German curriculum context, `country: "Germany"`,
stated goal "study Mechanical Engineering in Germany" (a genuinely Tier-3-target case, unlike
Persona E). Correct counselor behavior per this document: lead with Abitur-grade/NC-relevant
academic performance framing; any `leadership`/`community_impact`/`awards_distinction` guidance
should be explicitly reframed as "valuable for your development and for alternative pathways
(scholarships, dual-degree programs, non-NC programs, or if your plans shift toward a different
country), not for your primary NC-based admission" rather than implied to matter for the German NC
outcome itself. This is the clearest possible illustration of why §"what this means" point 2
matters: applying this package's other 10 documents to Persona H without this document's
conditioning would produce technically-accurate-but-strategically-misleading guidance.

## Rules established in this document

- `RULE-COUNSEL-061` — France's Parcoursup dossier includes activity/motivation elements weighted
  by program selectivity; treat as Tier 2 (meaningfully lighter-touch than the US, heavier than
  Germany/Switzerland). Confidence: high for mechanism, medium for exact relative weighting.
- `RULE-COUNSEL-062` — Germany's admission is Abitur-grade/NC-driven with a large open-admission
  category and no general extracurricular-activity factor found for the standard domestic pathway.
  Confidence: high for mechanism, medium for the open-admission-category's exact prevalence.
- `RULE-COUNSEL-063` — Dutch admission is diploma-based/open for most programs; numerus fixus
  programs use university-set decentralized selection that generally does not emphasize
  extracurricular activities specifically. Confidence: medium-high.
- `RULE-COUNSEL-064` — Italian public-university restricted programs (e.g. medicine, IMAT-track)
  are test-score-driven with no extracurricular factor; private institutions vary and may weight
  holistically. Confidence: medium (medicine-specific evidence; not verified across all fields).
- `RULE-COUNSEL-065` — Swiss admission is Matura pass/fail-gated for nearly all programs, including
  ETH/EPFL, with no activity review; medicine is the sole exception via a separate aptitude test,
  not an activity portfolio. Confidence: high (official ETH source).
- `RULE-COUNSEL-066` — Frame recommendations differently by target-system tier: "strengthens your
  application" language for Tier 1/2 targets, "supports your development / matters for [named
  alternative pathway]" language for Tier 3 targets, never implying a Tier-3 admission decision
  turns on activity evidence it structurally does not consider. Confidence: high (direct,
  reasoned consequence of the sourced tier findings).
- `RULE-COUNSEL-067` — For a Tier-3-track student, prioritize academic/exam-preparation
  recommendations as the primary admissions-relevant lever, without implying the other 8
  dimensions stop mattering for the student's actual development. Confidence: high.
- `RULE-COUNSEL-068` — Key this entire conditional off the student's *stated target*
  institution/system, never off nationality, residence, or curriculum alone — a student's origin
  must never be used as a proxy for which admissions system's rules apply to their goals.
  Confidence: high (direct application of the existing demographic-inference prohibition,
  peer-owned `RULE-COUNSEL-057`, to a new context).
