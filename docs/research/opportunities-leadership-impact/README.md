## Why this lane existed

A parallel lane tagged all 166 verified opportunities in ORYN's catalogue against the nine
student-development dimensions. The result:

- `community_impact` appeared as a base dimension **zero times** across 166 rows
- `leadership` appeared **twice**; `entrepreneurship` **twice**
- 87 of 166 rows were summer programs and 47 were competitions, which is why
  `intellectual_curiosity` (100), `career_exploration` (94), `academics` (61) and
  `awards_distinction` (47) dominate

That is an inventory problem, not a scoring problem. When ORYN's counselor correctly tells a
student "your biggest gap is leadership," it has nothing credible to recommend. This lane went
looking for the missing inventory.

## The five records that most directly close the gap

1. **Genç UPSHIFT** (Türkiye, Ministry of Youth and Sports + UNICEF, ages 15-29, all 81 provinces,
   free, 40 funded finalist teams). Uniquely among the social-entrepreneurship records it does
   **not** require an existing initiative — so it fits a student whose gap *is* leadership, rather
   than one who has already closed it. Ashoka and Three Dot Dash both require a running project as
   a precondition; recommending them to a student with a leadership gap is a category error the
   advisor should avoid.
2. **Habitat Derneği × UNICEF Train-the-Trainer** (Türkiye, ages 15-24, free, deadline
   **2026-08-26**). The organiser quantifies the obligation: "Organize at least 2 trainings every
   month" and "Reach at least 100 people by December 2026." Externally counted delivery, open to
   15-year-olds.
3. **Rotary Interact and Alpha Leo clubs** (global, ages 12-18). Chartered clubs with constitutionally
   defined, peer-elected officers, an Interact charter mandate of at least two service projects a
   year, and Leo elections escalating to district and multiple-district office. Present across
   Türkiye and Europe. Entirely absent from the catalogue before now.
4. **UK Youth Parliament** (ages 11-18). The only verified record involving a genuine contested
   election for a public office — manifesto, campaign, constituency vote, two-year term, ~300 seats
   nationally. Recorded for the *shape*, not to add UK volume.
5. **Girl Up Global Teen Advisor Board** (global, ages 13-19, $750 honorarium, 10-12 selected from
   hundreds). Appointed advisory-board seat with named operational committee duties and a 10 hr/month
   floor. The 2023-24 board included a Turkish advisor.

## Records with an honest downgrade

Four records are deliberately *not* labelled leadership, because the official pages do not support it:

- **İBB Genç Gönüllü** — 50 logged volunteer hours and a certificate. Real community impact with a
  municipal counterparty; no role, no team, no project. Only 3 of 15 placements accept under-18s.
- **Gençlik Merkezleri membership** — free, open, unselective. Infrastructure, not an achievement.
  Recorded because it is the delivery channel for Genç UPSHIFT across 162 centres.
- **European Youth Event** — a two-day gathering of ~9,000. Civic exposure, not leadership, and
  reported as first-come-first-served rather than selective.
- **Euroscola** — one day; the student's *school* wins a national competition. A credible civic
  distinction, not a position.

**THIMUN** is recorded as explicitly two-tier: attending in a school delegation is participation;
winning a Student Officer / specialised role is a real chair position. A CV line reading "THIMUN"
hides that entire distinction, and the advisor should ask which one.

## Conflicts recorded, not resolved

- **Peace First age range.** The organiser's current grants page says "typically ages 16–35."
  Numerous third-party listings and older Peace First materials say 13-25, with $250 mini-grants.
  Most likely the programme changed — but the page does not say so, so **no single age range was
  written**. The $250 figure was refused as a current fact for the same reason.
- **Girl Up cycle labels.** `/programs/teen-advisors` still advertises a superseded cohort with a
  January deadline while the linked application article describes the 2026-2027 board with an
  August 1 deadline. The two Girl Up pages disagree with each other. The article body was taken as
  authoritative and the staleness flagged. Girl Up records need more frequent re-verification than
  most — three of its pages carried stale cycle labels.
- **JA-family age rules.** JA Europe publishes 15-18 for the Company Programme; Young Enterprise UK
  publishes 13-19 for the same federation programme; INJAZ's Lebanese and Algerian members disagree
  about whether it is a high-school or university programme. These are genuinely different national
  implementations. No unified range was invented.
- **DofE Türkiye licence wording.** Third-party sources describe a 2013 "License Agreement" with the
  International Award Foundation; the operator's own page instead says TİKAV "is a member of The
  International Award Association." Different legal claims, left unmerged.
- **Young Enterprise page self-conflict.** Body text says 13-19; the page's own category tags say
  11-16 and 16-18. Body recorded, mismatch flagged.

## The `looksOfficial()` blocker — measured, not assumed

`lib/acquisition/source-authority.ts` treats a domain as official only on `.edu` / `.edu.` /
`.ac.` / `.gov` / `.gov.`, and the `opportunities` fact class is official-domain-or-nothing.

**Of 25 records, 2 pass that gate unmodified.** Both are Turkish government:
`genclikhizmetleri.gov.tr` (Genç UPSHIFT) and `e-genc.gsb.gov.tr` (Gençlik Merkezleri).

The other 23 would be rejected despite every one having been verified on the organiser's own page.
Rejected domains include `europa.eu` (the European Parliament and the European Commission —
Euroscola, EYE, Erasmus+), `ibb.istanbul` (an elected metropolitan municipality),
`istanbulkentkonseyi.org.tr` (a statutory municipal body whose site footer states it was built by
İBB's own IT department), `rotary.org`, `lionsclubs.org`, `young-enterprise.org.uk`,
`eyp.org.tr`, `intaward.org.tr` and `habitatdernegi.org`.

Every record therefore carries an **`organizer_domain_provenance`** field stating exactly how the
domain was tied to the organisation, so `sourceAuthority()`'s existing `officialDomains` parameter
can be populated from the data rather than by guesswork. Provenance quality varies and is stated
per record rather than uniformly asserted:

- **Government-corroborated (strongest):** `young-enterprise.org.uk` — GOV.UK's Department for
  Business and Trade directory names and links it. `istanbulkentkonseyi.org.tr` — the site's own
  footer credits İBB's Bilgi İşlem Dairesi Başkanlığı. `ibb.istanbul` — the municipality's own
  corporate site plus a matching `@ibb.istanbul` mail domain.
- **Institutional namespace:** `europa.eu` subdomains. These should simply be added to
  `officialDomains` outright; rejecting the European Parliament is a pure gate artefact.
- **First-party only, flagged as such:** `peacefirst.org`, `wearefamilyfoundation.org`,
  `schoolhouse.world`, `jaeurope.org`, `habitatdernegi.org`.
- **Partial, flagged with the specific gap:** `eyp.org.tr` (the international EYP site confirms EYP
  Türkiye as a National Committee but does **not** link the domain); `intaward.org.tr` (the parent
  Foundation's `/turkey` page returned no operator details, so the chain is asserted only from the
  Turkish end).

### A second, separate crawler blocker found in passing

Four organiser domains return **HTTP 403 to plain server-side fetchers** and could only be read
through a real browser session: `girlup.org`, `ashoka.org`, `lionsclubs.org`, `injazalarab.org`.
`istanbulkentkonseyi.org.tr` additionally presented an untrusted TLS certificate to server-side
fetch. This is independent of the domain-allowlist problem: even with a perfect `officialDomains`
list, an ingestion crawler using a plain HTTP client will silently fail on these five and quietly
under-represent exactly the .org youth organisations this lane exists to add. Noted in each
record's provenance field.

## Highest-value remaining gaps

Ordered by expected value to ORYN.

1. **Turkish gençlik meclisi electoral structure — the single biggest prize.** Search results
   indicate kent konseyleri are constituted under the Kent Konseyi Yönetmeliği, made under Article
   76 of Belediye Kanunu No. 5393, and that where a gençlik meclisi is formed its president sits
   *ex officio* on the kent konseyi's yürütme kurulu. If true, a gençlik meclisi başkanlığı is a
   statutory seat on a municipal executive body — the Turkish equivalent of the UK Youth Parliament
   shape, replicable across **dozens of Turkish cities** (Şişli, Sancaktepe, Ataşehir, Bursa and
   Tekirdağ all surfaced). **I could not verify it: `mevzuat.gov.tr` is unreachable from this
   environment by both server-side fetch and browser.** One person with access to mevzuat.gov.tr
   could turn one İstanbul record into a template covering ORYN's whole home market.
2. **Erasmus+ Türkiye Ulusal Ajansı call calendar.** The Erasmus+ Youth Exchange record is verified
   and Türkiye is a full participating country with an age floor of 13 and full funding — but call
   deadlines are set per National Agency and neither Commission page publishes one. Fetching the
   Türkiye National Agency's current round would make the highest-leverage record in the lane
   actionable rather than merely true.
3. **`gencupshift.org` retry.** Genç UPSHIFT is fully verified on a `.gov.tr` page but its cycle
   status is unknown; the programme site returned HTTP 522 (a server outage, not a removal). One
   successful fetch converts a CURRENT_CYCLE_NOT_PUBLISHED into a live, dated, free, nationwide
   Turkish record.
4. **TOG under-18 pathway.** Türkiye's best-known youth volunteering foundation, and no first-party
   evidence either way about school-age participation. A direct enquiry resolves it.
5. **MENA generally.** One unresolved record (INJAZ) for the entire region. INJAZ cannot be a single
   catalogue row — its national members publish conflicting eligibility — so it needs per-country
   research against each member's own site.
6. **Continental Europe beyond the EU institutions.** Everything European in this batch is either an
   EU institution or a pan-European federation. National youth councils, German/Nordic/Dutch youth
   foundations and municipal youth parliaments across Europe are unexplored.
7. **Turkish MUN circuit.** THIMUN is Dutch and expensive (€340/person plus travel). Turkish and
   regional MUN conferences with genuine chairing roles would be a far cheaper route to the same
   Student Officer shape.
8. **Peace First age range and grant amounts.** Needs a direct enquiry to the organiser; the
   conflict is unresolvable from public pages.

## A modelling note for whoever ingests this

Three records are **national operators of one canonical programme**, not independent opportunities:
DofE Türkiye (TİKAV) alongside the Australia and Hong Kong entries already in the catalogue; JA
Europe alongside the existing GençBizz and the new GençBizzTech and INJAZ. Ingesting them as
unrelated rows would triple-count the same underlying credential and distort any selectivity or
prestige signal. They should be linked as national operators of a parent programme.
