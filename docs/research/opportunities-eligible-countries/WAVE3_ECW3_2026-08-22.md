## The four compound/null-by-design findings, in detail

These are the batch's most substantive output even though none changes the database —
each documents a real structure that would be *misrepresented*, not just left incomplete,
by a flat country list:

- **AMC – AIME** (ECW3-001): genuinely tiered. AMC 10/12 and AIME are explicitly available
  **internationally** through MAA-licensed partners — the opposite of what the row's
  US-sounding subject suggests. The downstream USAMO/USAJMO/MOP/IMO team-selection tier

  the other; RULE-ELIGIBILITY-009 applies cleanly.
- **International Philosophy Olympiad** (ECW3-002): the row's own pre-existing description
  already correctly called this national-delegation-mediated. Fetched the full current
  57-country 2026 delegation list directly from the official site and recorded it as
  supporting evidence in the research record — but did **not** propose it as
  `eligible_countries`, because entry requires *winning your own country's national
  olympiad first*, not merely holding a passport from a listed country. Same shape as
  wave 1/2's EUCYS and Erasmus+ precedent.
- **International Young Physicists' Tournament** (ECW3-003): confirmed member-organization-
  mediated, and notably *not even strictly nationality-based* — a country with no existing
  IYPT body can found one and enter, which independently rules out a fixed country list
  regardless of the current roster.
- **Harvard Alumni for Global Women's Empowerment Essay Contest** (ECW3-019): gated by
  individual **school partnership**, not nationality. The organizer's own example list mixes
  countries with US cities/states (Turkey, Pakistan, Chicago, Connecticut, Los Angeles,

## The one prose-only proposal: Breakthrough Junior Challenge (ECW3-016)

Official rules state: *"If you reside in any country or region that is subject to
comprehensive U.S. economic sanctions or are otherwise an individual with which U.S.
persons cannot transact under U.S. law, you are not eligible to enter the contest."* This
is a real, sourced, explicit restriction — but it excludes by a **legal designation** (OFAC's
sanctions list, which changes over time) rather than naming a fixed set of countries on the
page itself. Inverting it into an "all countries except sanctioned ones" `eligible_countries`
array would be exactly the all-but-one-enumeration fabrication this project's standing rule
prohibits. Proposed as a `citizenship_restrictions` prose addition instead, quoting the rule
verbatim — `eligible_countries` stays null. Directly analogous to wave 2's Erasmus+ handling.

## A methodological refinement that emerged mid-batch: forward vs. backward framing

Several official pages carry language like "4,000 participants from all across the globe"
or "present in 65 countries" — international-sounding, but describing **who has attended**
(a scale/history claim) rather than **who can enter** (a forward eligibility claim).
Applied a consistent test across this batch: language that names the eligible population in
forward terms ("open to... any country," "students worldwide," "eligible schools around the
world") counts toward `confirmed_open_worldwide`; language that only describes past
attendance, organizational reach, or expansion history does not, and stays
`no_statement_found` even when it reads as internationally-flavored. This is the same
distinction wave 1 already applied to Bucket-2 keyword hits (descriptive ≠ eligibility) —
made explicit here because this batch hit it repeatedly (BmMT, HMMT, International Academic
Marathon, Nat Geo Slingshot, STEM Racing all fall on the backward/excluded side of the
line).
