## Findings worth a second look

- **Garcia Summer Research Program** (ECW4-002): a genuinely new *shape* of
  compound/null-by-design finding for this programme — gated by **immigration/visa status**
  ("international students may apply only if they already hold legal documentation to be
  in the U.S."; no visa sponsorship), not by nationality or a national-delegation mechanism
  like the wave-3 compound findings. A student of any nationality who already holds valid
  US status is eligible; the absence of sponsorship makes it impractical for most
  international applicants without already addressing nationality directly. Kept null with
  the mechanism documented rather than forced into either "restricted to X country" or
  "confirmed open."
- **Two "confirms open via addressed international-student requirements" cases** (Duke,
  ECW4-011; Freie Universität, ECW4-001; Parsons, ECW4-003): official pages that explicitly
  name non-domestic students as a defined category *with additional requirements*
  (different age floor, earlier deadline, English-proficiency test, visa) rather than
  excluding them. This is a stronger positive signal than a bare scale claim (the page is
  affirmatively addressing how an international student applies, not just claiming broad
  reach) — treated as `confirmed_open_worldwide`, distinguished in the notes field from the
  weaker "circumstantial" cases (Cornell, Georgetown, Harvard SSP all have visa-support
  infrastructure implying international attendance but no comparable explicit clause, and
  were kept at `no_statement_found`).
- **Iowa Young Writers' Studio and Johns Hopkins CTY** (ECW4-020, ECW4-021): both rows are
  specifically the **residential** track of a program that also runs a separate **online**
  track with different, more permissive language ("from anywhere in the world" for Iowa's
  online courses; "global community of peers" for CTY's online courses). Neither online
  statement was credited to the residential row it doesn't describe — flagged explicitly in
  each record's notes so a verifier doesn't miss the track mismatch if re-checking quickly.

## Method notes

- Same forward-vs-backward framing test as wave 3 (now an org-wide rule per BASORG),
  applied consistently: "students from 50 states and 87 countries" (BU), "come from more
  than 50 countries" (Cornell) — backward attendance claims, `no_statement_found`; "for...
  students...from all over the world" (Mathcamp), "welcoming teens from around the world"
  (Interlochen) — forward self-description, `confirmed_open_worldwide` at medium
  confidence.
- Circumstantial visa/international-office infrastructure (dedicated international-student
  pages, SEVIS/F-1 mechanics, "Resources for International Students" sections) was
  recorded as a genuine positive signal in several records' notes but **not** used alone to
  populate or confirm-open — held to the same explicit-statement bar as the rest of this
  project's research, distinguished from the stronger cases (Duke, Freie Universität,
  Parsons) where the page went further and explicitly addressed non-domestic applicants as
  an eligible category.
- Zero title/keyword prioritization — every row's own description/existing fields were
  read before any fetch; the "Canada/USA Mathcamp" name was specifically NOT treated as a
  citizenship signal (it is open worldwide despite the name — organizational/historical,
  not a gate), a direct instance of the Türkiye Scholarships-shaped trap this project has
  repeatedly guarded against.
