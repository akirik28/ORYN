# Computer Science — cross-country program-level requirements

Part of ORYN's R3.1 country-level admissions research package, layered on top of (not a
replacement for) each country's own admissions-architecture document in
[`../`](../). See [`../README.md`](../README.md) for the full ruleset (RULE-ADMISSIONS-001
through 017) and package context. Machine-readable version of everything below:
[`../../../../data/research/admissions-systems/program-requirements/computer-science.json`](../../../../data/research/admissions-systems/program-requirements/computer-science.json).
This document does not re-derive each country's general admissions architecture — it asks a
narrower question on top of it: for Computer Science *specifically*, what changes, and is the
change a genuinely different decision mechanism or just a higher bar within the mechanism that
country already uses? Four sibling documents in this package (Medicine, Law, Architecture,
Arts & Design) established the pattern and depth bar this document follows.

## Overview

Computer Science is the sharpest available test of whether "oversubscribed programme" always
means "structurally different" in this package, because global CS demand surged recently and on
top of admissions architectures mostly designed decades before anyone needed a CS-specific gate.
This package's Medicine document found a structurally different mechanism in 13 of 15 countries.
Computer Science inverts that ratio. Across the 15 countries researched here, Computer Science
runs through the country's own general admissions mechanism — the same threshold, formula,
ranking system, or points calculation used for any other popular degree — in 10 of 15 cases, with
selectivity expressed purely as an extreme, demand-driven cutoff (Turkey, Spain, Ireland, France,
Italy, Australia, New Zealand, Hong Kong, Singapore, Canada). This is the "numerically stricter
only" pattern this research brief anticipated as the dominant shape for a globally oversubscribed
field, and the anticipation held for two-thirds of the countries in this document — the majority
pattern, not a null result.

The genuine exceptions are informative precisely because they are a minority, and precisely
because none of them are CS-specific inventions — each rides an admissions mechanism the country
already had for other purposes, which Computer Science now happens to trigger because of demand.
**The United States** shows the sharpest and most consequential structural split: at a meaningful
subset of top public and private universities, Computer Science is not one admissions object but
either a direct-admit school-within-the-university evaluated separately and far more harshly at
the point of first application (UC Berkeley's EECS, Carnegie Mellon's School of Computer Science),
or a field a student can be admitted to the university without being admitted to, requiring a
separate, competitive, sometimes post-matriculation internal gate (Berkeley's own L&S/CDSS
Computer Science major, the University of Washington's Allen School). **The United Kingdom** and
**the Netherlands** each show a real but narrower structural difference: the UK layers a
mandatory admissions-test requirement (TMUA, plus an additional college-specific test at two
Cambridge colleges) onto general admission's grades-and-personal-statement model, though this
layer is shared with other quantitative subjects, not unique to CS; the Netherlands lets each
university independently decide whether Computer Science is numerus fixus at all, producing TU
Delft's tested, capacity-capped Computer Science & Engineering sitting alongside the University
of Amsterdam's uncapped Informatica for the identically-named field. **Germany** adds a third,
subtler version of the same idea: Computer Science never triggers Germany's heaviest national NC
architecture (reserved for exactly four subjects, none of them CS), but at the university level
it ranges from fully open (RWTH Aachen, confirmed zulassungsfrei) to a standard local-NC
grade-plus-criteria threshold (KIT) to a dedicated qualitative aptitude/suitability-assessment
procedure (TU Munich's Eignungsfeststellungsverfahren) — three different mechanisms for the
identically named field within one country. **Switzerland** supplies a useful negative-control
finding: Computer Science at ETH Zurich and most Swiss universities carries no numerus clausus of
any kind — the sole confirmed exception in Swiss admissions is human medicine — and CS's real
difficulty there is an informal, brutal first year, not a documented admissions gate.

## Country-by-country findings

### United States — structurally different, in at least two distinct shapes at once

Not one national mechanism, and not universal — this pattern concentrates at a subset of large
public flagships and a handful of elite privates where CS demand is highest; most US universities
simply let a student declare Computer Science as an ordinary major with no special gate. Where it
does apply, two genuinely different shapes were confirmed. **Direct-admit school-within-the-
university**: UC Berkeley's EECS is a College of Engineering major admitted directly and bindingly
at the point of freshman application (multiple sources place acceptance under 5%); Berkeley's own
EECS admissions page states that admissions processes for EECS/Electrical & Computer Engineering
(College of Engineering) versus Computer Science (College of Computing, Data Science and Society,
formerly under Letters & Sciences) "vary significantly" even though the technical CS coursework
is nearly identical. Carnegie Mellon admits every applicant into one of seven colleges at first
application, not to the university generally — the School of Computer Science (SCS) admits under
5%, versus roughly 24% for Dietrich College, the widest published gap between CMU's own colleges —
and CMU's own admissions materials confirm SCS is the only college requiring a mandatory SAT/ACT
score for Fall 2026 applicants while sibling colleges are test-flexible or test-optional, a
CS-specific policy layered on top of the already-separate-college mechanism. **Admitted-to-the-
university-without-admission-to-the-major**: Berkeley's own high-demand-major policy (which now
governs CS under CDSS) guarantees a place in a declared high-demand major only for students who
selected it on their initial application and then meet in-major requirements; a student admitted
via a different major or undeclared status gets just "one opportunity" to apply into CS later, at
"extremely limited" odds. The University of Washington's Allen School runs this same shape even
more explicitly: a "Direct to Major" pathway is evaluated at first application (Allen School's own
published data: roughly 2-5% of non-resident applicants, 26-37% of resident applicants), alongside
a wholly separate "Current UW Student Admission" pathway — its own departmental application, its
own CS-specific essay prompts, minimum prerequisite coursework graded 2.0 or higher — that remains
genuinely competitive (24% of Washington residents, 37% of non-residents/international applicants,
Spring/Autumn 2025 cycles per the Allen School's own admissions page) even though every applicant
is already a currently-enrolled UW student. **Not confirmed**: a CS-specific supplemental essay
prompt distinct from CMU's standard three-prompt set was not found in CMU's own admissions
materials — the broader "separately and more harshly admitted" claim about SCS is confirmed via
acceptance-rate and testing-policy evidence, but that specific narrower sub-claim is not.

### United Kingdom — structurally different, but the difference is shared with other quantitative subjects

TMUA (Test of Mathematics for University Admission) is mandatory for Cambridge Computer Science
and Imperial Computing for current-cycle entry — confirmed directly via both departments' own
admissions pages. Imperial's own course page confirms it no longer uses MAT (Mathematics
Admissions Test), which it required historically — a dated, confirmed change. TMUA is also used
by Warwick, Durham, UCL, and LSE for various quantitative courses (Mathematics, Economics, some
Statistics), so this testing layer is shared across quantitative subjects generally, not exclusive
to CS. Cambridge adds one further, narrower layer: the Computer Science Aptitude Test (CSAT) is
required only for applicants to two named colleges, Peterhouse and Trinity, as an *additional*
test sat on top of the university-wide TMUA requirement — confirmed directly via Cambridge's
Computer Science and Technology department's own admissions pages. This is a real difference from
general non-quantitative UK admission (predicted grades, one shared personal statement, one shared
school reference, rarely an interview) but should not be described as one Cambridge-wide "Computer
Science admissions assessment" replacing TMUA — it is TMUA university-wide, plus CSAT at exactly
two colleges.

### Netherlands — structurally different, and the mechanism itself varies within the field

Genuinely varies by university for the identical named field, confirmed for the current cycle.
TU Delft's Computer Science & Engineering is numerus fixus: 590 places for 2026-27 (345
English-track, 245 bilingual Dutch-English track), using a mandatory Mathematics + Systematic
Reasoning test to stratify applicants into ranked bands before a within-band placement decision.
The University of Amsterdam's Informatica, confirmed directly via UvA's own admissions page, is
**not** numerus fixus at all — subject only to UvA's standard "Matching" participation requirement
(mandatory, but not a competitive-selection gate; failing to complete it leads to deregistration,
not rejection). This is the same finding shape already established for Medicine in this package
(institution-varying selection method under the 2023 numerus fixus law), but the axis here is
starker: whether the field is capacity-restricted at all, not merely which method applies once it
is.

### Germany — structurally different, and split three ways at the university level

Computer Science never enters Germany's heaviest national mechanism — hochschulstart's
centrally-coordinated NC allocation applies to exactly four subjects (Medicine, Dentistry,
Veterinary Medicine, Pharmacy), and CS was not found on that list at any point in sources
reviewed. But at the university level, confirmed sources show three genuinely different
mechanisms for the identically-named Bachelor's field. RWTH Aachen's Informatik B.Sc. is confirmed
**zulassungsfrei** (admission-free, no local NC) for winter semester 2026/27, directly per RWTH's
own programme page. KIT's Informatik B.Sc. is confirmed **zulassungsbeschränkt** (443 places,
local NC) per KIT's own published programme flyer — structurally the standard Baden-Württemberg
local-NC shape (a grade-based criterion plus at least one non-grade criterion, per state
regulation). TU Munich's Bachelor Informatik uses neither of those: TUM's own programme page
states directly that student selection runs through an "**Eignungsfeststellungsverfahren**" — a
qualitative, multi-stage aptitude/suitability-assessment procedure, structurally distinct from a
plain grade-rank local NC and closer in kind to the Netherlands' decentralized-qualitative-
selection Medicine universities than to a typical German local NC. Fully open, standard local NC,
and a dedicated qualitative assessment procedure are three different mechanisms operating on one
field, at three of Germany's most CS-reputable universities, inside one country — none of it
touching the national NC system Medicine uses.

### Italy — numerically stricter only

Runs entirely through Italy's already-documented *local* numero chiuso track — never the national
one, which is reserved for Medicine, Dentistry, and Veterinary Medicine — using the same CISIA
TOL/TOLC-I test family used for many other high-demand degrees. Confirmed at Politecnico di
Milano (TOL test, minimum 30 points required, capped attempts per cycle) and Sapienza (TOLC-I,
English TOLC-I, or TOLC-CASA for its "limited/programmed admission" engineering degrees, which
include Informatica). Same mechanism and same instrument family as the general Italian
local-track pattern already documented generally — Computer Science is one of the fields that
reliably triggers it given demand, not a new mechanism.

### Germany-adjacent note: not applicable (see Germany above)

### Canada — numerically stricter only, with institution-level texture

Rides Canada's already-documented "separate faculty/programme application" model rather than
introducing anything new. University of Waterloo, the sharpest and most internationally-recognized
example, places Computer Science inside the Faculty of Mathematics (Cheriton School of Computer
Science) rather than Engineering: applicants submit an Admission Information Form (AIF) covering
courses, non-academic activities, and math-contest participation — confirmed directly via the
Cheriton School's own admissions page, contest participation (Canadian Computing Competition,
Euclid, Canadian Senior Mathematics Contest) is explicitly **not required** and mainly helps
borderline applicants — but, also confirmed directly, **no interview** is used for Computer
Science admission, unlike Waterloo Engineering, which layers an AIF plus a mandatory online
interview plus supplementary forms on top of its own five-course prerequisite set. This is
university-level texture inside Canada's existing general mechanism (the same "which faculty
administers this, and does it add an interview" variability the Canada country document already
established for Engineering generally), not a new structural category nationally.

### Switzerland — same as general admission (a confirmed negative-control finding)

The clearest disconfirming case in this document. ETH Zurich's own admissions materials and
swissuniversities' national reference both indicate no numerus clausus applies to Computer Science
(or almost any field) in Switzerland — the one confirmed exception across sources reviewed,
repeated consistently, is human medicine. A Swiss-Matura holder's route into ETH Zurich Informatik
is the same automatic-threshold model as any other Bachelor's field; a foreign-diploma holder sits
the same general ETH entrance examination (reduced or comprehensive, depending on prior
qualification) that any non-automatically-recognized applicant to any ETH field would sit —
nothing CS-specific about it. Computer Science's genuine, well-known difficulty at ETH is realized
almost entirely through informal first-year academic attrition rather than through any documented
admissions gate — a structurally different *kind* of "hard to get into" than every other country
in this document, and one that must never be modeled as an eligibility or selectivity fact the way
EMS or a numerus fixus quota is modeled for Medicine elsewhere in Switzerland.

### France — numerically stricter only

Runs through the two mechanisms the general France document already established, with no
CS-specific third mechanism found. Ordinary Licence Informatique at popular universities functions
as a "licence en tension" under the 2018 loi ORE — the same attendus-fit ranking layer already
documented for Droit/STAPS/Psychologie/Éco-gestion — now also reported at extreme oversubscription
(one cited example: roughly 5,600 Parcoursup applications for 145 places at a single Paris-area
university's Informatique programme). The elite quantitative track into engineering Grandes Écoles
runs through the same CPGE-to-concours mechanism already documented generally, using a
CS-flavored prépa track (MP2I in the first year, feeding into MPI in the second, alongside the
older MPSI/MP track) rather than a separate admissions architecture — a track label change, not a
new mechanism. MP2I/MPI selectivity varies enormously by lycée (low in the provinces, extreme at
Paris's most reputed CPGE classes), mirroring the country document's own finding that CPGE
selectivity is genuinely local, not nationally uniform.

### Spain — numerically stricter only

Runs through the identical ponderación/nota de corte/Distrito Único mechanism used for every other
public Grado programme, with an extreme, self-adjusting cutoff at the most in-demand schools:
Ingeniería Informática's illustrative 2025-26/projected 2026 cutoffs sit above roughly 10.5 out of
14 at both UPC's Facultat d'Informàtica de Barcelona and UPM — a range comparable to several
public Medicine cutoffs documented in this package's Medicine research. No dedicated test,
portfolio, or interview layer was found for the public track — the same mechanism as any other
competitive Spanish Grado, just with an extreme emergent cutoff.

### Ireland — numerically stricter only

Runs entirely through ordinary CAO Leaving Certificate points, with no HPAT-style second
instrument, portfolio, or interview found for Computer Science at either Trinity or UCD — despite
Computer Science carrying some of the highest points requirements in the country (Trinity's
Computer Science course reported by Trinity's own school as the highest-points CS degree in
Ireland in the 2025 cycle; UCD Computer Science required 542 points in Round 1 of the same cycle,
both well above 500 and approaching the 625-point maximum). The Higher Level Mathematics
+25-point bonus that applies generally across the Irish system is the only Mathematics-specific
lever available, not a Computer-Science-specific one — and CAO's own "restricted-application
course" category (used for Medicine, Art & Design/Architecture-type portfolio courses) does not
name Computer Science.

### Australia — numerically stricter only

Runs through the same floating ATAR/selection-rank mechanism used for the large majority of
Australian programmes, with published guide ATAR figures spanning roughly 75-82 at mid-tier
universities up to 85-95 for Advanced Computing-style degrees at Group of Eight universities. No
Commonwealth-Supported-Place-style fixed government place allocation (the specific mechanism that
makes Medicine categorically different in Australia) and no UCAT ANZ/ISAT-style dedicated
admissions test or interview was found for Computer Science in sources reviewed. Mathematical
Methods is commonly a named prerequisite or strongly assumed-knowledge subject at Go8
universities, consistent with the general prerequisite/assumed-knowledge distinction already
documented for Australia broadly — not a CS-specific mechanism.

### New Zealand — numerically stricter only

Runs through the same Rank Score/grade-average threshold mechanism used for most New Zealand
Bachelor's programmes, with quantitative programmes commonly expecting Level 3 Calculus and/or
Physics from the shared national UE-approved-subjects list. No UCAT ANZ-style dedicated test, and
no two-stage gateway-then-competitive-progression model of the kind confirmed for NZ Medicine, was
found for Computer Science in sources reviewed this pass.

### Hong Kong — numerically stricter only (confirmed at HKUST; HKU unresolved)

Sits inside the same JUPAS/non-JUPAS bifurcation already documented generally in this package
(RULE-ADMISSIONS-013): every internationally-educated applicant, Turkish or otherwise, is
categorically routed to non-JUPAS regardless of field. Within non-JUPAS, HKUST's own FAQ for
non-JUPAS/non-local applicants states directly that an interview "is not a requirement" for its
School of Engineering, which houses Computer Science — a confirmed absence at HKUST specifically,
unlike Medicine's confirmed Multiple Mini Interview/panel requirement at HKU and CUHK
respectively. HKU's own Computer Science admissions page does not itself specify whether an
interview applies to non-JUPAS CS applicants; treat HKU CS's interview status as an unresolved
gap rather than assuming either Medicine's pattern or HKUST's confirmed absence transfers to it.

### Singapore — numerically stricter only (the sharpest same-country contrast with Medicine in this document)

This package's Medicine document confirmed NUS gates Medicine (and Dentistry, Law) with both a
mandatory ranking-position rule (the field must be a top-1-or-2 ranked choice on the application)
and a compulsory standardized-test layer (UCAT, or SAT/ACT-with-Writing plus AP Exams) that most
NUS programmes do not carry. Computer Science carries neither. NUS Computing is evaluated through
the same holistic-assessment mechanism (a structured achievements list, short-response questions)
used for the large majority of NUS programmes, and NTU's Computer Science (College of Computing
and Data Science) is evaluated through the same Integrated Grade Profile (IGP) mechanism used
across NTU generally, with H2 Mathematics as a subject-level expectation rather than a dedicated
test. Both are extremely selective by demand — one ranking aggregator cites NUS Computer Science
as the single most difficult NUS programme by admission difficulty (secondary-sourced, not an NUS
figure); NTU CS's 10th-percentile IGP sits in the AAB/C band — but the admission *mechanism*
itself is the ordinary one used for most programmes, not Medicine's added ranking-gate-plus-test
layer. SMU's specific CS mechanism was not independently confirmed to the same depth.

### Turkey — numerically stricter only, confirmed exactly as anticipated

Bilgisayar Mühendisliği is placed entirely through the standard YKS SAY score-type mechanism — the
same formula, the same national rank-order competition, the same absence of interview or
portfolio, as any other SAY-track engineering programme. What makes it "restricted" is a cutoff
near the top of the national distribution at the most reputable programmes: illustrative 2026-cycle
figures place Boğaziçi's, Koç's, and Bilkent's Computer Engineering programmes within roughly the
top 300-600 nationally by success ranking. No CS-specific mechanism of any kind was found — the
cleanest confirmation of "numerically stricter only" in this document, matching Turkey's own
Medicine finding in this package's Medicine document.

## What determines eligibility

For the 10 "numerically stricter only" countries, eligibility for Computer Science collapses to
exactly the same fact that determines eligibility for any other competitive programme in that
country: the qualifying secondary credential, plus whatever subject-weighting/prerequisite/bonus
mechanism that country already applies generally (Spain's ponderación bonus subjects, Ireland's
HL Maths +25 bonus, Turkey's SAY score-type routing, Australia's prerequisite-vs-assumed-knowledge
Mathematics distinction). Nothing distinctively "CS" enters the eligibility calculation in these
countries beyond an extreme cutoff. For the 4 structurally different countries, eligibility stacks
an additional, CS-triggered fact: which specific university/college a student applies to in the US
(determining whether a separate, harder admissions pool applies at all, or whether university
admission alone is sufficient to enter the major); a qualifying TMUA score, and at two Cambridge
colleges a qualifying CSAT score, in the UK; which Dutch university a target Informatica/CSE
programme sits at (determining whether numerus fixus applies at all); and which German university
a target Informatik programme sits at (determining whether the mechanism is open enrollment,
standard local NC, or TUM's dedicated aptitude procedure). Switzerland is the one country
confirmed to require nothing beyond the same automatic Matura-threshold or entrance-exam fact any
other non-Medicine Bachelor's field requires.

## What genuinely strengthens an application

Where a testing layer exists, preparation for that specific instrument is the highest-leverage
lever found: TMUA performance in the UK (and CSAT performance at Peterhouse/Trinity specifically),
TU Delft's Mathematics + Systematic Reasoning test in the Netherlands, and strong performance in
TUM's Eignungsfeststellungsverfahren in Germany. In the US's direct-admit shape (Berkeley EECS,
CMU SCS), demonstrated depth in mathematics/CS coursework and rigor is the clearest lever found —
the Allen School's own published admit profile names AP Computer Science A, AP Calculus BC, and
demonstrated programming experience (competitions such as USACO, hackathons, open-source
contributions, research) specifically. Math-contest participation (Canadian Computing Competition,
Euclid, Canadian Senior Mathematics Contest) helps at Waterloo, though the Cheriton School's own
page is explicit that contest results are not required and mainly help borderline applicants.
Where the mechanism is purely numerical (the 10 "stricter only" countries), the only lever
identified anywhere in this pass is the same one that helps for any other competitive programme in
that system — maximizing the exact grade/points/score formula already documented for that country
generally — with no CS-specific evidence category found.

## What is largely irrelevant

General extracurricular breadth unconnected to demonstrated computing/mathematics depth carries no
confirmed weight anywhere in this document, including in the four structurally different
countries — even the US's more holistic processes (the Allen School's own description of its
evaluation) weight programming/competition/research evidence specifically, not activity count.
Essays and recommendation letters are absent as CS-specific levers everywhere they are already
absent from that country's general admission model (Netherlands, Germany, Italy, Spain's public
track, France's licence-en-tension/CPGE mechanisms, Turkey). CMU is a partial exception worth
stating precisely: CMU requires its standard three-essay set of every applicant including SCS
applicants, but no SCS-*specific* essay prompt beyond that shared set was confirmed in sources
reviewed, so this should not be treated as a CS-specific lever distinct from CMU's general
application. Contest results are explicitly non-required at Waterloo, and general "leadership"
framing (the kind that helps a broad US activities list generally) was not found as a meaningful
factor for Computer Science specifically anywhere in this document.

## What ORYN must never infer

Never assume a country's Computer Science mechanism mirrors its own Medicine mechanism, in either
direction — Singapore is the sharpest confirmed example of the two pointing opposite ways
(Medicine structurally gated with a ranking-position rule and mandatory test; CS running the
ordinary holistic/IGP process), but the caution applies generally: a country appearing in
Medicine's "structurally different" list (Ireland, Australia, New Zealand, Hong Kong, France) is
not thereby likely to show a structurally different CS mechanism, and the reverse also holds
(Germany and the Netherlands are structurally different for both fields, but via different
specific mechanisms in each case). Never treat "EECS" and "Computer Science" — or any two
similarly CS-adjacent major names at the same US university — as interchangeable or as sharing one
admissions pathway; Berkeley's own site states its admissions processes for the two "vary
significantly," and the specific rate, deadline, and eligibility facts for one do not transfer to
the other. Never assume a US applicant admitted to a university has thereby been admitted to that
university's Computer Science major — this is true at only a subset of US universities
(concentrated at large public flagships and a few CS-elite privates), never assume it generalizes
nationally, and always check whether the specific target university uses direct-admit-by-major, a
declared-major guarantee, or fully open in-major declaration. Never assume a German, Dutch, or
Italian university's Computer Science admission mechanism generalizes to a different university in
the same country for the identical field — confirmed variation (open at RWTH vs. NC at KIT vs.
aptitude-assessed at TUM; numerus fixus at TU Delft vs. uncapped at UvA) means the country-level
fact alone is close to unusable for this field. Never repeat the CMU School-of-Computer-Science
separate-supplemental-essay claim as confirmed fact — it was a lead this research was asked to
verify, and it was not found in CMU's own admissions materials. Never model Switzerland's genuine
CS difficulty as an admissions-eligibility fact the way EMS or numerus fixus is modeled for Swiss
Medicine — it is a post-entry academic-attrition phenomenon, a categorically different kind of
"hard," and modeling it as an eligibility gate would misrepresent the actual mechanism.

## Institution-specific vs. programme-specific vs. country-wide patterns

**Country-wide, uniform**: Turkey's YKS-SAY-only mechanism (no CS-specific layer at any Turkish
university); Ireland's CAO-points-only mechanism (uniform across every HEI reviewed, no
CS-specific second instrument anywhere); Spain's ponderación/Distrito Único formula (uniform
mechanism nationally, though the specific weighted subjects are set per university per degree);
the structural fact, not the specific numbers, that Switzerland exempts Computer Science from any
numerus clausus almost everywhere.

**Programme/track-specific within one country**: France's licence-en-tension-vs-CPGE-MP2I/MPI
split (the same two underlying general mechanisms already documented, applied to two different
CS-adjacent tracks); the UK's TMUA-plus-college-specific-CSAT split (university-wide TMUA, but
CSAT only at Peterhouse and Trinity); Canada's Faculty-of-Mathematics-vs-Engineering split at
Waterloo specifically (AIF alone for Computer Science, AIF-plus-interview for Engineering, at the
same institution).

**Genuinely institution-specific, varying even within one field in one country**: the
Netherlands' numerus-fixus-or-not split (TU Delft capped, UvA uncapped, for the identically-named
field, same 2026-27 cycle); Germany's three-way mechanism split (RWTH open, KIT standard local NC,
TUM dedicated aptitude assessment, all confirmed for the current cycle); the United States' entire
structural pattern, which is inherently institution-specific by design — Berkeley's
EECS-vs-CS-vs-Engineering-Undeclared split, CMU's SCS-vs-six-other-colleges split with its own
CS-specific mandatory-testing policy, and UW's Direct-to-Major-vs-Current-UW-Student split, none
of which describe any other US university and none of which should be treated as representative
of "how US Computer Science admission works" generally.

The practical implication for ORYN: unlike Medicine, where a "restricted programme" flag is close
to universally true across this package, a Computer Science requirement data model needs a
country-level default of "runs through the general mechanism, just stricter" that a much smaller,
specific set of confirmed university-level overrides (not just cutoff overrides) can replace —
collapsing the US, UK, Netherlands, and Germany findings into the same generic "restricted
programme" bucket used for Turkey or Spain would erase the actual mechanism difference a
counselor needs to act on.

## Unresolved questions

Germany: whether TUM's Eignungsfeststellungsverfahren-based Informatik selection is representative
of other TUM CS-adjacent programmes (e.g. Data Engineering and Analytics, Games Engineering) or
specific to the core Informatik Bachelor's was not checked this pass; whether KIT's Informatik
local-NC criteria include a specific non-grade component beyond the Baden-Württemberg-mandated
general minimum was not independently confirmed for Informatik specifically. United States:
whether Carnegie Mellon's School of Computer Science carries any CS-specific supplemental essay
prompt beyond CMU's standard shared three-essay set — a specific sub-claim in this research's
original brief — was not confirmed in CMU's own admissions materials reviewed this pass; treated
as unconfirmed rather than verified. Hong Kong: whether HKU's Computer Science non-JUPAS pathway
requires an interview, the way Medicine does at HKU and CUHK and the way HKUST's Engineering
School confirms it does not for Computer Science, was not conclusively confirmed from HKU's own
admissions pages this pass. Singapore: SMU's specific Computer Science admission mechanism was not
independently researched to the same depth as NUS's and NTU's this pass — SMU's general "SAT 1350
for most non-IB international qualifications" rule (documented in this package's country document)
is assumed to apply by default, but no SMU-CS-specific added mechanism was checked for. Netherlands:
the full national list of which Informatica/Computer-Science-adjacent Bachelor's programmes beyond
TU Delft (capped) and UvA (uncapped) currently use numerus fixus for the 2026-27 cycle — e.g.
Eindhoven University of Technology, Radboud, VU Amsterdam, Utrecht — was not exhaustively mapped
this pass. Turkey: İTÜ's and METU's specific 2026-cycle Bilgisayar Mühendisliği cutoff ranks were
not directly confirmed this pass (Boğaziçi, Bilkent, and Koç were); their inclusion among
"top-tier, near-maximum cutoff" programmes rests on general reputational tier and the confirmed
pattern at peer institutions, not independently verified rank data for these two specifically.
Australia: whether any Group of Eight university runs a CS-specific admissions test, portfolio, or
interview layer (as opposed to a pure floating ATAR/selection-rank threshold) was not found in
sources reviewed this pass — read as a gap given this pass's depth, not a confirmed absence.
Ireland: whether any Irish HEI outside Trinity and UCD runs a CS-specific test or interview layer
was not checked directly — treated as likely-absent by pattern (CAO's own "restricted-application
course" category names Medicine and Art & Design/Architecture-type portfolio courses but not
Computer Science), not exhaustively confirmed across every HEI offering the field.
