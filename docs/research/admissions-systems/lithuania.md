# Lithuania — undergraduate admissions system

Part of ORYN's R3.1 country-level admissions research package (see [`README.md`](./README.md)
for the cross-country matrix, source standard, and ruleset). Added 2026-09-03, second entry from
the corridor re-measurement's own long-tail list (after [`estonia.md`](./estonia.md)) — same
scope discipline: one research session, official/institutional sources, unresolved questions
listed rather than guessed. Lithuania has 4 institutions in ORYN's database with zero admissions
depth.

## A. A genuinely mixed mechanism, not a clean rank-vs-holistic split

**Different in kind from every rank-competitive country in this expansion line (Sweden, Greece,
Poland).** Lithuania's centralized system computes a numeric competitive score — structurally the
same family as those countries — but that score has a real, defined, bounded channel for
non-academic evidence, on both the domestic and international sides. This is not a case of
"mostly grades, occasionally an exception"; it is a real formula with a real motivation/interview
component built in by design. Classified `holistic_review` for both pathways on that basis,
matching this registry's own precedent for a scored-but-genuinely-holistic system (the original
15's Singapore/NUS entry already treats a structured, points-weighted achievements component as
holistic rather than as a rank-competitive bonus).

## B. Domestic — LAMA BPO's competitive score, with a real bounded motivation component

**LAMA BPO** (Lietuvos aukštųjų mokyklų asociacija bendrajam priėmimui organizuoti — the
Association of Lithuanian Higher Education Institutions for Centralised Admissions), authorized
by Lithuania's Ministry of Education, Science and Sport, runs the single centralized admission
system ("Bendrasis priėmimas," general admission) for essentially all Lithuanian higher education
institutions, in main and additional stages. LAMA BPO computes a competitive score per applicant.
Confirmed: **"a motivation assessment of 0–1.5 points is added to the competitive score
calculated by LAMA BPO,"** as one of several additional-points components whose combined total
"cannot exceed 2.5." This is a genuine, defined, non-academic-evidence channel into a centrally
computed score — not a subject-matter exam, not extra credit for a specific school type, an
actual assessment of the applicant's motivation. Its exact size relative to the base academic
component (drawn from secondary-school subject results) was not confirmed this pass — see
"Unresolved questions" — but its *existence*, as a real, named, points-denominated component, is
primary-sourced.

## C. International (non-EU/EFTA) — direct to each university, interview-centric

Non-EU/EFTA applicants generally apply directly to each Lithuanian university rather than through
LAMA BPO, in July. Confirmed across multiple sources: a short motivational interview — live
online or pre-recorded, university's choice — is typical, with "the interview grade included in
the overall competitive score," assessing motivation, English proficiency, self-expression, and
programme knowledge. Some universities additionally request a roughly 300-word motivational essay
("not every educational institution requires one" — a real, sourced caveat against generalizing
this to all of them). Entrance exams are generally *not* required for English-taught Bachelor's
programmes, though "certain fields... may ask students to complete a test, portfolio, or
interview" on top of the standard process. A five-year cap between completing the prior
qualification and applying was found for non-EU/EFTA applicants specifically — a real eligibility
constraint, distinct from the selection mechanism itself.

## Standardized tests

Not generally required for English-taught programmes on the international track. No SAT/ACT role
found on the domestic LAMA BPO track; the competitive score is built from Lithuanian secondary
subject results plus the bounded additional-points components described in section B.

## Language requirements

Not independently verified to a specific numeric threshold this pass for either track; the
interview itself is described as partly assessing English proficiency for international
applicants presenting an English-taught application.

## Essays / recommendations / extracurriculars

Confirmed present and structurally load-bearing on both tracks, not merely possible: LAMA BPO's
own bounded motivation-assessment component domestically; a graded interview (and, at some
universities, a ~300-word essay) internationally. This is the basis for the `holistic_review`
classification on both sides — the strongest, most explicitly-scored version of "a real channel
exists" found anywhere in this expansion line.

## Safe inferences

Lithuania's centralized LAMA BPO system computes a numeric competitive score, but that score
includes a real, named, points-denominated motivation-assessment component — not just academic
results. The international track is more explicitly interview-driven (a graded interview folded
into the same kind of competitive score concept), with entrance exams generally not required for
English-taught programmes specifically. Both tracks genuinely read something beyond the academic
record, even though both are still ultimately expressed as a computed, ranked score rather than
an unscored US-style holistic file review.

## Unsafe inferences

Do not assume the 0–1.5 motivation-assessment points dominate or are negligible relative to the
base academic score — the proportional weight was not confirmed this pass, only the component's
real existence and its own stated cap. Do not assume every Lithuanian university requires the
international-track essay — one source explicitly says not all of them do. Do not assume entrance
exams are never required internationally — "certain fields" is a real, if unspecified, exception
to the general "not required for English-taught Bachelor's" finding.

## Counselor actions

For a domestic/LAMA-BPO-track student, explain that the competitive score is not grades-only —
prepare for the motivation-assessment component as a real, if bounded, part of the score, not an
afterthought. For a Turkish or other non-EU/EFTA applicant, treat the interview as the central
preparation task, not a formality — it is graded and folded into the competitive score exactly
the way LAMA BPO's motivation component is domestically. Confirm with the specific target
university whether a written essay is also required, since this genuinely varies. Flag the
five-year qualification-to-application gap explicitly for any student who took time between
secondary school and applying.

## Unresolved questions

The exact proportional weight of the motivation-assessment component relative to the base
academic score (LAMA BPO's own full scoring methodology was not independently primary-fetched
this pass). Which specific fields require an additional test/portfolio/interview internationally,
beyond the general finding that some do. Numeric language-proficiency thresholds. Current-cycle
deadlines beyond the general "July" figure found for the international/direct-application track.
Whether any restricted/high-demand field (Medicine, for instance) layers additional criteria on
top of either track.
