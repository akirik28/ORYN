# Portugal — undergraduate admissions system

Part of ORYN's R3.1 country-level admissions research package (see [`README.md`](./README.md)

not folded into the README's 15-country cross-country matrix.

**Trigger:** continuation of the founder-requested corridor expansion. Like Sweden and Norway,
Portugal was picked because ORYN's database already holds institutions there with zero
admissions depth (`shape: "unknown"` in every outlook computation until this entry lands).

## A. Admissions architecture — two officially named, structurally different competitions

Portugal runs two separate, both officially named, tracks — not a wording difference like
Sweden, and not an "unofficial" institutional split like Norway's English-taught track. DGES
(Direção-Geral do Ensino Superior — Portugal's Directorate-General for Higher Education, the
national authority) runs the **Concurso Nacional de Acesso** (National Access Competition) for
domestic and EU/EEA-national candidates, and a legally separate **Concurso Especial para
Estudantes Internacionais** (Special Competition for International Students) for candidates
holding "International Student Status" (Estatuto de Estudante Internacional, IES — established
nationally by Decree-Law 36/2014). Both are DGES-recognized national frameworks; they are not
the same mechanism wearing two names.

## B. Concurso Nacional de Acesso (domestic/EU track) — rank-order, numerus clausus

**Confirmed directly from DGES's own English-language page.** Placement is rank-order until each
institution/course pair's fixed places run out: "the placement in each institution/course is
made according to the ordered lists of candidates until the available places are exhausted."
Ranking uses the **nota de candidatura** (candidacy grade), combining secondary-school results
and national exam ("prova de ingresso") scores — DGES's own overview page confirms the
existence and rank-order use of this grade and states each institution/course sets its own
minimum thresholds on both the entrance exams and the candidacy grade, but does not itself state
the exact secondary-school-vs-exam weighting formula, which was not independently retrieved this
pass. **No essay, interview, reference letter, or other non-academic evaluation criterion is
mentioned anywhere in this mechanism.**

## C. Concurso Especial para Estudantes Internacionais (international track) — nationally defined population, institution/faculty-set selection

**A genuine, well-evidenced "varies below the country level" finding — confirmed more granularly
than Norway's equivalent finding, not merely inferred.** The national legal framework
(Decree-Law 36/2014, plus each university's own implementing regulation — the University of
Lisbon's is Dispatch 10579/2019) establishes **who counts** as an International Student (broadly:
non-Portuguese, non-EU/EEA nationals, subject to exceptions this pass did not fully map) and
that this population competes through a separate track from the Concurso Nacional. It does
**not** establish a single national selection mechanism within that track. DGES's own
international-students page states directly: "each university or polytechnic institution has
different application procedures depending on study program... different admission
requirements, different deadlines and different documents." University of Porto's own page
narrows this further, to **below the institution level**: "each faculty of U.Porto establishes
its own admission conditions" under the shared IES regulation. University of Lisbon's own
central page confirms the same structure (one legal framework, faculty-set criteria), while one
specific Lisbon faculty (ISCSP) was found to use a named foreign national exam (Brazil's ENEM)
"for ranking purposes" for applicants holding it — a real, sourced example of a rank-style
mechanism existing at that specific faculty for that specific applicant population, but too
narrow a data point to generalize into "ISCSP is rank-based" for international admission overall,
let alone the rest of University of Lisbon or Porto.

No institution-wide override is recorded in the registry entry this document supports, unlike
Norway's NTNU exception — the evidence found this pass shows variation at a finer grain
(faculty, and even applicant-nationality-within-faculty) than a clean single-institution
override could honestly represent.

## Standardized tests

None found playing a role in either track beyond Portugal's own national "provas de ingresso"
(entrance exams), which are themselves the input to the domestic nota de candidatura, not a
supplementary aptitude test in the SAT/ACT sense. No SAT/ACT role was found in the international
track either, beyond the one ISCSP/ENEM example above (a different country's national exam, not
a US-style aptitude test).

## Language requirements

Confirmed to exist for the international track (University of Lisbon's page requires candidates
to be "independent users of the course's teaching language") but no specific numeric IELTS/TOEFL
thresholds were independently retrieved and verified this pass — flagged as a gap. No language
requirement applies to the domestic track beyond ordinary Portuguese secondary schooling.

## Essays / recommendations / extracurriculars

No confirmed requirement in the domestic Concurso Nacional mechanism — explicitly grades/exam-
only per DGES's own page. Not confirmed present or absent as a general rule for the
international track, since that track's actual selection criteria are institution/faculty-set
and were not exhaustively surveyed this pass; no source reviewed described an essay or reference
requirement at any of the three institutions checked (DGES general page, Porto, Lisbon).

## Safe inferences

Portugal's domestic/EU track is a centralized, DGES-run, rank-order numerus clausus system with
no non-academic evidence channel — the same general shape as Spain's nota de admisión and
Sweden/Norway's domestic tracks already in this registry. The international track is a
nationally *defined* population (Decree-Law 36/2014's International Student Status) but a
locally *decided* selection process — confirmed to vary at least by institution and, at the
University of Porto specifically, by faculty within one institution. This is a genuinely
different shape of variation from Canada's (which varies by institution but was not confirmed to
go finer than that) and from Norway's (one named, confirmed institution-wide exception).

## Unsafe inferences

Do not assume the domestic nota de candidatura formula (the exact secondary-school-vs-exam
weighting) — its existence and rank-order use are confirmed, its precise weighting is not. Do
not assume any single Portuguese institution's or faculty's international-track criteria from
another's — Porto's own page explicitly warns against exactly this by faculty, not just by
university. Do not generalize ISCSP-Lisbon's confirmed ENEM-ranking mechanism to any other
faculty, applicant nationality, or the rest of the University of Lisbon's international
admission. Do not assume Portugal's international track is holistic just because it isn't
rank-based like the domestic track — no essay/reference/interview requirement was actually
confirmed anywhere in the sources reviewed; "unknown" here means genuinely unresolved, not "we
checked and it's holistic."

## Counselor actions

For an EU/EEA-national or Portugal-resident student, explain the Concurso Nacional's rank-order,
no-essay mechanism plainly — the nota de candidatura and the national exams are the entire
decision. For a non-EU international applicant (the realistic case for most ORYN users), do
**not** promise a specific selection mechanism — confirm the target faculty's own published
criteria directly, since DGES's own guidance is that this varies by institution and, at some
universities, by faculty. Where a national exam the student already holds (e.g., a Brazilian
ENEM score) might be usable at a specific faculty the way it is at ISCSP-Lisbon, verify with that
specific faculty rather than assuming the same option exists elsewhere.

## Unresolved questions

The exact nota de candidatura weighting formula (secondary-school average vs. national exam
score, and whether this varies by course). Numeric language-proficiency thresholds
(IELTS/TOEFL-equivalent) for the international track. Whether any restricted/high-demand course
(Medicine, for instance) layers an additional mechanism on top of either track. A representative
survey of international-track selection criteria across more than the three institutions/pages
checked this pass — genuinely unresolved whether most Portuguese faculties lean toward a
rank-based, threshold-based, or holistic evaluation for their international applicants; this
pass found real variation but not a dominant pattern.
