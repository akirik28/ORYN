# Estonia — undergraduate admissions system

Part of ORYN's R3.1 country-level admissions research package (see [`README.md`](./README.md)
for the cross-country matrix, source standard, and ruleset). Added 2026-09-03, continuing the
same single-country expansion line as [`sweden.md`](./sweden.md) through
[`belgium.md`](./belgium.md) — same scope discipline: one research session, official/
institutional sources, unresolved questions listed rather than guessed, not folded into the
README's 15-country cross-country matrix. First entry from the small in-corridor "long tail"
identified by the corridor-scope re-measurement, not the original founder-supplied candidate
list — Estonia has 3 institutions in ORYN's database with zero admissions depth.

## A. Two platforms, and — unlike most of this expansion line — a genuine shape split confirmed on primary evidence for one side only

Estonia splits by applicant category at the **platform** level: **SAIS** (Sisseastumise
Infosüsteem) for Estonian citizens and long-term residents, **DreamApply** for everyone else —
confirmed as a shared platform across the University of Tartu, TalTech, Tallinn University, and
most other Estonian institutions (one account, one document set, multiple applications). This
maps onto the domestic/international pathway split this registry already uses.

## B. International (DreamApply) — confirmed holistic, primary-sourced

Confirmed directly from Tallinn University's own official admissions page, for non-EU/EEA
bachelor's applicants: "the admission exam may consist of more written assignments and **always
includes a video interview**." Programme-specific additions layer on top — "programme-specific
requirements may also include a written assignment and Curriculum Vitae." Separately, prior
education is evaluated by the university's own admission specialists, who may consult the
Estonian ENIC/NARIC for qualification assessment. A mandatory interview, confirmed for every
applicant regardless of programme, is unambiguous: this is `holistic_review`, not a credential
check with an occasional add-on. Corroborated more broadly (secondary sources, motivation-letter
guides): most Estonian programmes are described as requiring a motivation letter as standard
practice, not a competitive-programme exception — consistent with, not contradicting, the
primary-sourced interview finding.

## C. Domestic (SAIS) — genuinely unresolved at a general level, not forced

**Could not establish one general mechanism, and the evidence found points toward genuine
institution/programme-level variation rather than a gap in this pass's search effort.** Estonia's
state examinations (riigieksamid, three exams on a 100-point scale, sat as part of secondary
graduation) "serve as entrance examinations for higher education institutions" — but multiple
sources agree specific requirements are "set by the higher education institution and depend on
the chosen field of study," and that "tests, interviews, portfolios etc. may be required in
addition" to state exam results, without saying for which institutions or fields. Both the
University of Tartu's and Tallinn University's own pages, when asked about SAIS-track evaluation
specifically, deferred to "see the web page of the particular curriculum" rather than stating a
general rule. This is a real "unknown," but held to a slightly lower confidence than this
package's comparable Czechia finding: Czechia's divergence was confirmed by one institution's own
page explicitly contrasting two different mechanisms ("some programmes X, others Y"); Estonia's
is inferred from multiple sources independently declining to generalize, which is suggestive but
not as directly demonstrative. Flagged honestly as the weaker of the two "genuine divergence"
findings in this expansion line, not presented at Czechia's confidence level.

## Standardized tests

No SAT/ACT role found in either track. Riigieksam results function as the domestic evidentiary
base (see section C); no equivalent standardized instrument was found required generally for the
DreamApply/international track beyond the admission exam/interview itself.

## Language requirements

Confirmed for the international track: commonly IELTS 6.0 / TOEFL iBT 72 for both BA and MA
programmes, per general Study in Estonia guidance — not independently verified against a specific
university's own page to primary-source confidence this pass.

## Essays / recommendations / extracurriculars

Confirmed present and standard for the international track (motivation letter, interview,
sometimes CV/written assignment). Not established either way for the domestic track — genuinely
unresolved, consistent with section C's overall finding, not asserted absent.

## Safe inferences

Estonia's international/DreamApply track is genuinely holistic — a mandatory interview for every
non-EU/EEA applicant, confirmed from Tallinn University's own page, not inferred from a
secondary-source generalization. The domestic/SAIS track has no single describable mechanism in
the sources reviewed; state exam results feed in, but specific evaluation is set per institution
and field, with tests/interviews/portfolios appearing "in addition" for some (unspecified) cases.

## Unsafe inferences

Do not assume Estonia's domestic track is purely grades/exam-based just because riigieksamid
exist and are described as "entrance examinations" — the same sources naming that also say
additional institution-set requirements, including interviews and portfolios, apply for some
fields. Do not assume the international track's mandatory-interview finding, confirmed at Tallinn
University specifically, generalizes to every Estonian institution's exact process without
checking — though the DreamApply platform being shared across institutions makes this more likely
to hold broadly than in, say, Czechia's case, it was primary-confirmed at one institution, not
surveyed across all of them.

## Counselor actions

For a Turkish or other non-EU/EEA applicant, prepare for the international track as genuinely
holistic — a real interview is expected, not just paperwork, and a motivation letter should be
treated as a real, substantive document rather than a formality. For an Estonian
resident/citizen, do not promise a grades-only mechanism — confirm the specific target
programme's own stated requirements, since Oryn could not establish a general rule and multiple
official sources say it varies by field.

## Unresolved questions

Whether any general domestic (SAIS) mechanism exists that this pass's search simply didn't
surface, or whether the variation is genuinely as unstructured as multiple sources' deferrals
suggest. Which specific fields/institutions require tests/interviews/portfolios on the domestic
track, and which don't. Whether the international track's mandatory-interview finding holds
identically at every DreamApply-participating institution or varies in ways Tallinn University's
own page doesn't reflect. Numeric language-proficiency thresholds, confirmed against a primary
source. Current-cycle deadlines.
