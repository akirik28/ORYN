# Counseling Implications

What ORYN should actually tell a Turkish student, and why. Every claim here traces to a rule in documents 01–05; nothing new is asserted without a source there.

---

## The single most important finding

**Turkish central university placement contains no holistic component whatsoever.**

A student's placement score is exactly:

```
placement score = weighted exam score + (OBP × 0.12)
```

That is the whole model (`RULE-TR-EXAM-014`). There is no essay. No personal statement. No recommendation letter. No interview. No activity list. No portfolio. No demonstrated interest. No consideration of leadership, volunteering, competitions, internships, research or entrepreneurship. ÖSYM places by score into quota, breaking ties with OBP and then date of birth.

**This inverts ORYN's default advisory posture for a large share of its home market.** The product's core loop — identify a profile gap, recommend an activity to close it — is calibrated for US-style holistic admissions. Applied unmodified to a YKS-track student, it recommends activities that carry **literally zero weight** in the outcome the student cares about, while consuming the hours that would move their rank.

For a YKS-track student, telling them to "build a well-rounded profile" is not merely unhelpful. It is **advice that makes their stated goal less likely**, because the time is zero-sum against test preparation. ORYN must not give it.

### The narrow, real exceptions

Three routes let non-exam achievement touch a Turkish placement outcome. All are narrow, and ORYN should present them as such rather than as a general opening:

1. **International science olympiad medals** — 1st place adds OBP × 0.06, 2nd × 0.05, 3rd × 0.04 to the placement score for relevant programs (`RULE-TR-EXAM-014`). This is an *international olympiad podium*, not a school science fair. For the handful of students genuinely in contention it is real; for everyone else it is not a plan.
2. **Özel yetenek (talent) programs** — sport, music, art, and some teaching programs admit via ÖYSP, where `YP = (0,55 × ÖYSP × 5) + (0,12 × OBP) + (0,45 × TYT-P)`. Here practised skill genuinely dominates. Relevant only if the student's target is a talent program.
3. **Named categories with their own provisions** — school valedictorians (okul birincileri), martyrs'/veterans' families, TÜBİTAK competition winners, national athletes. These are status-based, not profile-based.

Outside these, extracurricular achievement does not enter Turkish central placement.

### When profile-building *is* right

The advice must branch on the student's actual target, not on their nationality:

| Target | Does profile-building help? |
|---|---|
| Turkish university via YKS | **No** — zero weight (bar the exceptions above) |
| **IB / AP / IGCSE program inside a Turkish school** | Only via **UDSP score**, which is a language exam — so "profile" means language ability |
| Universities abroad (US/UK/EU) | **Yes** — this is where ORYN's default model applies |
| Turkish university via international quota | **Per-institution** — must be sourced per university (`RULE-TR-EXAM-023`) |

Many ORYN users will be **dual-track** — hedging between YKS and applying abroad. That is the hardest and most common case, and it deserves an honest statement of the trade-off rather than a pretence that one plan serves both. The two systems reward genuinely different things, and hours spent on one are not spent on the other.

## Where effort actually moves the outcome

### YKS: TYT is chronically under-prioritised

TYT is **40% of every score type** — SAY, SÖZ, EA and DİL alike (`RULE-TR-EXAM-010`). Within TYT, Türkçe and Temel Matematik are 33% each.

For a medical applicant (SAY):

| Component | Share of final SAY score |
|---|---|
| TYT Türkçe | **13.2%** |
| TYT Temel Matematik | **13.2%** |
| AYT Matematik | 30% |
| AYT Fizik | 10% |
| AYT Kimya | 10% |
| AYT Biyoloji | 10% |

**TYT Türkçe alone outweighs Physics, Chemistry or Biology individually.** A student grinding AYT Fizik while neglecting TYT Türkçe has the trade-off backwards, and this is a common and expensive error. ORYN can state this with confidence because it comes straight from Tablo 1C and 1E.

### Mathematics is where marginal effort buys the most rank

Official 2026 means (`02-yks-and-obp.md` §3): TYT Temel Matematik **6.4 / 40**, AYT Matematik **7.1 / 39**. Compare TYT Türkçe at **20.1 / 40**.

Because scores are standardised on the standard deviation, gains where the distribution is *thin* move rank further than the same gains where candidates are densely packed. Most candidates sit near the floor in maths. Moving from 6 to 12 net on TYT maths is a much larger rank movement than moving from 20 to 26 net on Türkçe.

ORYN should present this as **what the 2026 distribution looked like**, never as a prediction or a guaranteed rank gain.

### Guessing strategy differs by exam — and students transfer the wrong habit

| Exam | Penalty | Correct strategy |
|---|---|---|
| **UDSP** | none | **Answer every question. Blanks are pure loss.** |
| **LGS** | wrong ÷ 3 (4 options) | Blind guess EV = 0; guess if you eliminate ≥1 option |
| **YKS** | wrong ÷ 4 (5 options) | Blind guess EV = 0; guess if you eliminate ≥1 option |
| **TR-YÖS** | wrong ÷ 4 | Same as YKS |

The dangerous transfer is a student carrying YKS's "don't guess" caution into UDSP, where it costs them real marks for nothing. On UDSP, 70 is **exactly 35 of 50 correct** (`RULE-TR-EXAM-006`) and unanswered questions can only hurt.

### OBP: small, slow, and impossible to fix late

The OBP contribution spans **30 to 60 points** — a 30-point swing (`RULE-TR-EXAM-014`). It is built from the diploma grade accumulated across all four high-school years.

This makes it an unusual lever: **low salience, low per-week cost, and completely irreversible near the end.** A grade-9 or grade-10 student can still shape it cheaply. A grade-12 student in March cannot. ORYN should raise OBP maintenance *early*, when it is still actionable, and stop raising it once it isn't — continuing to surface it to a final-year student is noise.

Note also that OBP is computed **Turkey-wide with no school adjustment** (`RULE-TR-EXAM-013`). A student at an academically demanding school is not compensated for tougher grading. That is worth saying plainly, because students at selective schools often assume otherwise.

## Deadline-linked actions ORYN should actually surface

These are dated, irreversible, and easy to miss — the highest-value thing a deadline engine can carry. Dates below are 2026-cycle; **the pattern recurs, the dates do not carry forward.**

| When | Action | Why it's irreversible |
|---|---|---|
| **YKS application (Feb)** | Declare DKAB vs. extra Philosophy on form field 25 | Marks in the wrong block are discarded on exam day, no remedy (`RULE-TR-EXAM-008`) |
| **UDSP application (May)** | Apply — fee must be paid *before* applying | No application without prior payment |
| **~3 weeks before UDSP (5 June)** | File accessibility accommodations via school → RAM | Deadline falls *after* the application window closes |
| **YKS results / tercih (July)** | **Do not list a program you would not attend** if considering a re-sit | Being *placed* halves OBP coefficient next year — "kayıt olsun veya olmasın" (`RULE-TR-EXAM-015`) |
| **23–30 June, 16:00** | Verify diploma grade and education record in ÖSYM AİS | Corrections only via school/e-Okul; ÖSYM disclaims all responsibility afterwards |

The **tercih trap** deserves special emphasis. A student who lists a safety program, wins it, declines it, and re-sits next year loses up to 30 placement points — and nothing at registration time warns them, because the trigger already fired at placement. ORYN is well positioned to catch this precisely because it knows the student's intentions before the deadline. This is arguably the single highest-value warning the product can deliver to a Turkish user.

## What is largely irrelevant

Say so plainly rather than staying silent — ruling things out is as valuable as recommending things (`PHASE 39`, "don't do this" logic):

- **YDS, e-YDS, YÖKDİL** — postgraduate and academic-career exams. A secondary student should not take them (`RULE-TR-EXAM-021`).
- **ALES, DGS** — require a bachelor's and an associate degree respectively. Structurally impossible now (`RULE-TR-EXAM-024`).
- **TR-YÖS, for anyone schooled in Turkey** — no such route exists; YKS is the only path (`RULE-TR-EXAM-023`).
- **LGS, for anyone already in high school** — fixed history. Context, never an action item.
- **Extracurricular breadth, for a YKS-only student** — zero weight. Do not recommend it as profile-building.

## What ORYN must never infer

A consolidated list of the fabrication risks this research surfaced:

1. **Never convert a prior-year fact into a current-year fact.** The 2026 UDSP date moved by a week *after* first publication. 2027 dates, fees and formats for every exam here are unpublished.
2. **Never quote a net→rank or net→score conversion as a forecast.** YKS standardisation is against each year's final-year cohort; the mapping shifts annually (`RULE-TR-EXAM-009`).
3. **Never assume a fixed question denominator.** 2026 AYT scored Matematik over 39 of 40 and TDE over 23 of 24 after cancellations.
4. **Never state a CEFR level or skills breakdown for UDSP.** MEB publishes neither.
5. **Never say UDSP gates Abitur.** No MEB primary source names it (`RULE-TR-EXAM-001`).
6. **Never treat 70 on UDSP as sufficient.** It is a floor; oversubscribed programs rank by score (`RULE-TR-EXAM-003`).
7. **Never apply a school-quality adjustment to OBP.** The regulation forbids it explicitly.
8. **Never confuse the two OBPs.** YKS's is added to the score; LGS's is a tie-break only (`RULE-TR-EXAM-016`).
9. **Never present Tablo 1G ranks as sufficient for a specific program.** They are national floors; foundation universities may set stricter ones.
10. **Never convert YDS → an international exam score.** Equivalence is one-way only (`RULE-TR-EXAM-022`).
11. **Never claim IELTS carries a YDS equivalence.** It is absent from ÖSYM's closed list — while *also* not claiming Turkish universities reject it, which is a separate per-institution question.
12. **Never generalise one country's credential recognition to another.** Only the Netherlands was verified (`RULE-TR-EXAM-025`).
13. **Never present TR-YÖS as required for international admission.** Universities set their own methods.
14. **Never infer age from grade level.** UDSP eligibility is defined by grade and enrolment, never age; repetition, early enrolment and the denklik route all break the mapping.

## Suggested advisory posture

When ORYN meets a Turkish secondary user, the first thing to establish is **which system they are actually optimising for** — because the correct advice diverges almost completely:

- **YKS-only** → test-score mechanics, weight-aware time allocation, OBP maintenance if early enough, deadline hygiene. Explicitly de-prioritise activity building.
- **Abroad-only** → ORYN's default holistic model applies.
- **International program inside Turkey (IB/AP/IGCSE)** → UDSP is the immediate gate; maximise the score rather than clearing 70.
- **Dual-track** → name the trade-off honestly. Do not imply one plan optimises both.

This is exactly the kind of prioritisation and opportunity-cost reasoning the product specification calls for — and Turkey is the market where getting it wrong is most visible, because the scoring formula is fully public and contains no room for the things a US-style advisor would recommend.
