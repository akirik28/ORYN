# International Routes and Credential Recognition

Researched 2026-08-21. Covers three things ORYN needs to keep strictly separate:
1. **TR-YÖS** — the route *into* Turkish universities for students educated abroad.
2. **ALES / DGS** — exams that exist but are **not** for our users.
3. **The MEB diploma abroad** — how a Turkish high-school diploma is treated outside Turkey.

---

## 1. TR-YÖS — `RULE-TR-EXAM-023`

**Türkiye Yurt Dışından Öğrenci Kabul Sınavı**, run by ÖSYM. Held twice a year. Verified from the official English-language 2026-TR-YÖS/1 guide (35pp).

2026 cycle: **TR-YÖS/1** — applications from 14 January 2026, fee deadline 4 February 2026. **TR-YÖS/2** — applications 9 July – 3 August 2026 (extended to 11 August), **exam 11 October 2026**. Applications via `https://tryos.osym.gov.tr`.

> The 11 October 2026 sitting is **still ahead** as of this research date. Its application window has already closed. This is the only exam in this package with a live upcoming date.

### Who may take it — and, crucially, who may not

The guide's threshold condition, before any of the sub-clauses:

> "Applicants — provided that they are in their final year of upper secondary education (high school) or have graduated, and **excluding those enrolled in or graduated from upper secondary (high school) institutions in Türkiye** — who: …"

The eligible categories are then:
- **(a)** foreign nationals;
- **(b)** former Turkish citizens who renounced citizenship with Interior Ministry permission, or hold a **Blue Card**;
- **(c)** those who acquired Turkish citizenship later, or hold dual nationality in that status;
- **(ç)** TRNC citizens who completed **all** of their high school education in a foreign country;
- **(d)** **Turkish citizens who completed their entire high school education abroad** (excluding TRNC schools, but including MEB-established Turkish schools abroad outside the TRNC), **and who can certify they were physically present in that country during the corresponding years.**

Carve-outs to the "studied in Türkiye" exclusion, for categories (a)–(c): **embassy schools**, **international private educational institutions listed in MOBİS**, and foreign nationals in Türkiye under MEB/YÖK projects.

### The rule ORYN must never get wrong

> "As of 2026, the Council of Higher Education has decided that citizens of the TRNC, persons who have renounced Turkish citizenship/Blue Card holders, and foreign nationals who have enrolled in and/or graduated from secondary education institutions in Türkiye … may be admitted to higher education institutions in Türkiye and in the TRNC **solely through the Higher Education Institutions Examination (YKS)**, and placement shall be made via YKS."

**A student who did their high school in Turkey has no YÖS route into Turkish universities. YKS is the only path.** This holds regardless of nationality, and it holds for dual nationals and Blue Card holders. Turkish citizens qualify only via route (d) — *entire* high school abroad, with residence certification.

For ORYN's primary user — a Turkish student at a Turkish high school — **TR-YÖS is not an option, and presenting it as an alternative to YKS would be actively misleading.** It becomes relevant only for a genuinely internationally-schooled user.

### Format and scoring

- **Two tests: Numerical Aptitude (40 questions) and Basic Mathematics (40 questions). 100 minutes total.**
- Offered in **Turkish, German, English, French, Russian and Arabic**.
- **Raw = correct − (wrong ÷ 4)** — same quarter penalty as YKS.
- Standardised to mean 50 / SD 10, then weighted **0.45 Numerical Aptitude + 0.55 Basic Mathematics**, rescaled to **100–500**.
- A weighted score requires **≥0.5 raw in at least one** of the two tests.
- **Valid for 2 years** from the exam date.

Note what is absent: **no Turkish-language test, and no verbal/reading component at all.** TR-YÖS is entirely quantitative. Language proficiency is handled separately by each university.

### TR-YÖS is not the only route in

> "Methods and principles to be used in student admission to higher education institutions, such as **countries' own national exams, international exams, and exams administered by specific universities**, as well as their lowest scores, will be specified on the web pages of the relevant higher education institution."

Universities set their own admission methods for international quota and may accept national exams (e.g. a national baccalaureate), international qualifications (e.g. IB, SAT, A-Level) or their own entrance exams. Students apply **directly to the university**. ÖSYM explicitly disclaims responsibility for housing, scholarships and residence permits.

**ORYN must therefore never state that TR-YÖS is required for international admission to a Turkish university.** Requirements are per-institution and must be sourced per institution.

## 2. ALES and DGS — not for our users — `RULE-TR-EXAM-024`

The task brief asked these be flagged clearly rather than padded out. They are both ÖSYM exams, and both are **structurally impossible** for a 14–18 secondary student:

| Exam | Full name | Prerequisite | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ALES** | **A**kademik **P**ersonel ve **L**isansüstü **E**ğitimi Giriş **S**ınavı | Holding, or being about to complete, a **bachelor's (lisans) degree** | Entry to postgraduate study and academic staff posts |
| **DGS** | **D**ikey **G**eçiş **S**ınavı | Graduating from a **meslek yüksekokulu** or open-education **associate (ön lisans)** program | "Vertical transfer" from a 2-year associate degree into a 4-year bachelor's program |

2026 DGS was held **19 July 2026** and consists of a numerical and verbal aptitude test.

**Neither can be taken by a secondary student, and neither should ever appear in ORYN's recommendations for one.** If a Turkish user asks about ALES or DGS, the correct response is that these come *after* a university degree (ALES) or *after* an associate degree (DGS) — years beyond their current stage.

DGS is worth one line of forward-looking context: it means a student who ends up in a 2-year *ön lisans* program has a documented, centrally-run route into a bachelor's program later. That is a genuine fact about the system's permeability, not a current action.

## 3. The MEB diploma abroad — `RULE-TR-EXAM-025`

The brief was explicit: **do not invent equivalencies; record only what official credential-recognition sources actually state.** I checked one, and I am reporting only that one.

### What I verified: the Netherlands

**Nuffic** is the Dutch national credential-evaluation organisation (the Dutch ENIC-NARIC). Its published comparisons for Turkish credentials:

| Turkish credential | Nuffic's stated Dutch comparison | NQF-HETR / EQF |
|---|---|---|
| **Lise Diploması** | "**at least a HAVO diploma**" | not assigned |
| Meslek Lisesi Diploması | "an MBO diploma, level 2 or 3" | not assigned |
| Teknik Lisesi Diploması | "a HAVO diploma for the general education part" | not assigned |
| Ön Lisans Diploması | "an associate degree or 2 years of HBO" | 5 / 5 |
| Lisans Diploması | "an HBO bachelor's degree or a WO bachelor's degree" | 6 / 6 |
| Yüksek Lisans Diploması | "an HBO master's degree or a WO master's degree" | 7 / 7 |

Note the exact wording for the general high-school diploma: **"at least a HAVO diploma."** That is a floor, not an equation. Nuffic does **not** state it equals VWO (the Dutch university-preparatory track), and ORYN must not upgrade the phrasing. Any claim stronger than Nuffic's own words is fabrication.

Nuffic performs these comparisons under the **Lisbon Recognition Convention**, whose operative principle is that a foreign diploma is treated as the same level as the domestic one **unless there are substantial differences**.

### What I did NOT verify — and what ORYN must not claim

**One country is not "abroad."** I checked Nuffic only. I did **not** verify:

- **UK ENIC** (UK) — the authoritative UK statement; its comparability material is subscription-gated.
- **ANABIN / KMK** (Germany) — which is particularly consequential, since German recognition of a Turkish *Lise Diploması* has historically involved additional requirements (e.g. Studienkolleg or a qualifying university-entrance result) rather than direct admission.
- **US** — where there is **no national credential-recognition authority at all**; evaluation is done by private agencies (WES, ECE and others) and, in practice, by each university's own admissions office.
- **France, Italy, Switzerland, Canada** or any other market in ORYN's stated scope.

**ORYN must not generalise the Nuffic finding to any other country.** Credential recognition is jurisdiction-specific, is often program-specific *within* a jurisdiction, and frequently carries extra conditions (language proficiency, a foundation year, a minimum diploma grade, specific subject coverage) that a one-line "comparable to" statement does not capture.

The honest product behaviour: show the Netherlands comparison with its source and its exact wording, and show **"not yet researched"** for every other country — rather than interpolating.

Status: Netherlands `VERIFIED_CURRENT`. All other jurisdictions `UNVERIFIED`.
